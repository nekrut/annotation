## EST evidence: endpoint and validation caveats

Wei and Brent (2006), [Using ESTs to improve the accuracy of de novo gene prediction](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-327), Table 1 and Figures 2–4: worm sensitivity uses 4,705 confirmed WS130 genes; precision includes only predictions overlapping confirmed annotations by at least one base. This is not genome-wide precision. Table 1 reports percentages:

| Condition | Gene sensitivity | Gene precision | Exon sensitivity | Exon precision |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| TWINSCAN 2.03 | 60.6 | 58.6 | 86.9 | 79.5 |
| TWINSCAN_EST, all EST evidence absent | 61.3 | 59.8 | 86.2 | 80.8 |
| TWINSCAN_EST, evaluated genes' EST evidence masked | 63.0 | 60.5 | 86.4 | 81.1 |

Local masking improves gene sensitivity/precision by 2.4/1.9 percentage points while exon sensitivity falls 0.5 points. The model encodes absent and conflicting evidence identically; absence can itself penalize gene predictions. **Inference:** an empty evidence channel need not reproduce the original DNA/comparative model.

Methods use eight-fold genomic-fragment cross-validation for WS130, but no cross-validation for the GAZE test, whose parameters came from all confirmed WS130 genes. Thus GAZE's reported 73%/62% gene sensitivity/precision does not establish independent transfer. The human comparison masks pseudogenes for every method before evaluation; distinguish it from unmasked N-SCAN results. [Open full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2105-7-327).

## Pseudogene filtering: gene gains and exon losses

Van Baren and Brent (2006), [Iterative gene prediction and pseudogene removal improves genome annotation](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.4766206), Table 1: human build 35 with mouse informant, evaluated against 13,133 CCDS annotations. Percentages below are exact gene/ORF sensitivity and precision, and exon sensitivity. External parents use known transcript/protein databases; bootstrap parents use N-SCAN predictions.

| N-SCAN condition | Predicted genes | Gene sensitivity | Gene precision | Exon sensitivity |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Unmasked | 24,712 | 37.0 | 19.7 | 84.8 |
| PPFINDER, external parents | 21,736 | 37.9 | 22.9 | 85.1 |
| PPFINDER, bootstrap parents | 21,511 | 37.3 | 22.8 | 84.3 |

Bootstrap gene precision rises 3.1 percentage points, but exon sensitivity falls 0.5 points. PPFINDER incorrectly flags 37 CCDS annotations; the authors identify 23 as likely functional retrogenes. Its intron/exon-length filter is explicitly unsuitable for short-intron species such as C. elegans; it also excludes nonprocessed pseudogenes from its target class. **Recommendation:** audit functional retrogenes and exon losses when adding pseudogene controls; do not import this mammalian filter unchanged across clades. [Open publisher PDF, Results and Discussion](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/16/5/678.full.pdf).
