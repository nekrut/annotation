## Comparative evidence helps, but transfer was not species-held-out

Gross et al. (2007), [CONTRAST](https://doi.org/10.1186/gb-2007-8-12-r269), [open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2246271/), Tables 1 and 5, reports the following exact coding-gene sensitivity/precision percentages. These are authors' measurements, with any reference coding isoform eligible to match; UTRs are excluded.

| Target/reference | N-SCAN comparator | CONTRAST best single informant | CONTRAST multiple informants |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Human hg18, February 2007 CCDS | mouse: 35.6 / 25.1 | mouse: 50.8 / 29.3 | reported 11: 58.6 / 35.5 |
| Fly dm2, filtered RefSeq | three informants: 59.7 / 46.4 | D. ananassae: 63.1 / 48.3 | reported 13: 66.1 / 52.7 |

The human multiple-informant increment over CONTRAST with mouse is **7.8 percentage points sensitivity and 6.2 points precision**, calculated from Table 1. This isolates a more relevant comparison than attributing the entire N-SCAN-to-CONTRAST improvement to additional genomes. It still compares separately trained configurations, not a fixed model receiving more rows at inference.

Methods split known gene annotations randomly into four folds, predict the full genome for each fold, sum correct predictions for held-out annotations, and use the mean number of predictions across folds as the precision denominator. A separate internal split assigns 1-Mb genomic fragments to SVM training, CRF training and holdout in proportions 25%/50%/25%. Holdout performance selects regularization and early stopping; SVM hyperparameters use examples from the CRF training set. One randomly selected transcript per gene trains the model. **Inference:** distinguish held-out annotations, internal model selection and species transfer; these experiments do not establish a universal fixed model. The separate EGASP experiment excludes ENCODE regions from training and is another benchmark, not the same split.

The informant-selection experiment greedily improves boundary-classifier error on balanced positive/negative examples. It excludes the unstable GC-donor classifier and does not repeat the greedy selection with full-gene accuracy because global training is expensive. Therefore its reported saturation after six informants is not an established optimum for exact-gene recovery. All details above: the paper's Human/Fly results, Informant set, Cross-validation and Training sections.

## Geometry and constraints that a new model must make explicit

CONTRAST's trimer-pair features are indexed by informant row, and its quadratic SVM features refer to particular row/position combinations. Reverse-complement sharing is explicit, but taxon permutation invariance is not. **Inference:** being phylogeny-free does not make the released formulation species-independent; swapping rows without corresponding parameter changes can change its scores. A shared variable-taxon encoder would need a different representation or parameter-sharing rule. Source: Feature mapping and Coding region boundary classifiers.

The decoder uses posterior boundary rewards rather than selecting the maximum-probability CRF path. Its reward is `kappa*p - (1-p)`, with `kappa=1` for human and `1.5` for fly, selected after observing better performance. Algebra gives local reward sign crossings of `p=0.5` and `0.4`; these are **not independent acceptance thresholds**, because the final path must satisfy the gene grammar. **Inference:** record decoding choices as part of a comparator, in addition to weights. Source: Maximum boundary accuracy decoding.

The stated grammar allows GT/GC donors and AG acceptors and carries intron substates to exclude internal stop codons formed across exon junctions. Yet the fly reference filtering allows AT donors and AC acceptors as well. **Inference:** a reference transcript requiring an AT-AC junction cannot be exactly reproduced by the stated grammar. Its prevalence and any implementation exception remain unmeasured; this is not a claimed numerical recall ceiling. Source: Global model of gene structure and Fly results.

## Two documentary inconsistencies

1. The Results prose says sensitivity and precision improve for every exon type. Table 2 instead gives single-exon precision **27.7% N-SCAN, 18.8% single-informant CONTRAST and 24.8% multiple-informant CONTRAST**. The last value is **2.9 percentage points below** N-SCAN. Single-exon sensitivity does improve; preserve both endpoints rather than repeating the universal precision claim.
2. The fly paragraph and Table 5 say **13 informants**, but the paragraph lists **14 assembly identifiers**: droSim1, droSec1, droYak2, droEre2, droAna3, dp4, droPer1, droWil1, droVir3, droMoj3, droGri2, anoGam1, apiMel2, triCas2. Preserve the reported table label and flag the discrepancy. The exact experiment membership requires original input/configuration recovery; the inconsistency alone does not invalidate the scores.
