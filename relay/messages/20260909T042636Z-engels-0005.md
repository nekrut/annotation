## ClaMSA: recover the cross-clade results, preserving the version

The [open March 2021 preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.03.09.434414), sections 3.3 and 4.1–4.3, and its [supplement](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2021/03/10/2021.03.09.434414/DC1/embed/media-1.pdf?download=true), Table 1, supply the following author-reported AUC values:

| Training / method | Vertebrate test | Fly test | Yeast test |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ClaMSA, mixture of all three clades | 0.992 | 0.971 | 0.979 |
| ClaMSA, vertebrates only | 0.992 | 0.950 | 0.695 |
| ClaMSA, flies only | 0.991 | 0.979 | 0.736 |
| ClaMSA, yeast only | 0.891 | 0.795 | 0.946 |
| ClaMSA LogReg, mixed training | 0.988 | 0.961 | 0.943 |
| PhyloCSF, native clade model | 0.945 | 0.905 | 0.956 |

The preprint's test sets contain 3,000 vertebrate, 3,000 fly and 1,000 yeast candidate alignments. Frame and strand are supplied. Its candidate construction uses AUGUSTUS-CGP; negative examples that overlap reference CDS in the same frame and strand are removed. Negative lengths are subsampled toward the positive length distribution, and examples are randomly partitioned. **Inference:** this evaluates a selected candidate-classification problem; it does not measure same-frame boundary errors, complete genes, or performance under natural genome-wide prevalence. Recalibrate probabilities before interpreting them as genome-wide coding probabilities. The described random split does not establish a locus- or gene-family-disjoint evaluation.

Animal-to-animal transfer is strong in this experiment; animal-to-yeast transfer is substantially worse. Mixed-clade performance includes training data from every tested clade. **Inference:** these results support a comparative baseline, while leaving truly unseen-clade transfer unresolved.

Supplement section 1.7 separately reports mixed-training three-matrix AUCs of 0.991, 0.967 and 0.974 for vertebrates, flies and yeast. This is a matrix-count ablation with the other defaults retained, not the LogReg row above. The tree-construction example in section 1.5 reports roughly 15 hours using four MrBayes MPI processes, with hardware unspecified; this is preprocessing, not classifier inference time.

**Version warning:** the recovered main PDF is explicitly dated March 10, 2021. Its abstract reports a sixfold false-positive reduction, whereas the [2022 journal abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35060608/) reports fourfold. Do not attach these preprint tables to the [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btac028) as verified journal-version results. Journal table reconciliation and absolute classifier runtime remain pending.

## ANNEVO: distinguish preprint, published supplement, and current release

The [April 2025 preprint](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-6402260/v1), Methods, describes separate clade models and sequence-conditioned expert weighting, plus category/phase/transition heads. It reports a 20 kb central window with 10 kb flanks. Those specifications are version-specific.

The freely downloadable [published supplement](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41592-026-03036-7/MediaObjects/41592_2026_3036_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) changes the architectural description: Method 2 specifies convolutional compression, Transformer layers, sparse expert routing, resolution restoration and five output classes. Note 16 explicitly identifies five separately trained clade models. Note 14 attributes 60.6% of its investigated false positives to fragmented mRNAs, including introns called intergenic. Thus long-intron handling remains a failure mode despite the long-gene examples.

The same supplement says training typically takes within a week on four V100S GPUs (Method 2). Runtime totals combine GPU prediction and CPU decoding (Note 4). Tiberius comparisons used memory-dependent adjustments, including CPU fallback; its GPU runtime comparison excluded many mammalian species (Note 8). Those comparisons cannot be treated as one uniformly configured, matched-panel speed ratio. These are author reports, not measurements this tick.

Current source is pinned to [42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/tree/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff). The [training README](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/model_training/README.md) describes a sequence-only pipeline with normal/longer total inputs of 40,960/128,000 bp. It is not evidence that the historical paper used the newer longer input.

The [benchmark notes](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/docs/notes.md) identify ANNEVO 2.3.0 and Tiberius 2.0.4. They retain the longest reference transcript for exon scoring and any matching isoform for locus scoring; invalid start/stop and intron-length cases are filtered. They disclose training-species overlap for comparators. This is a practical released-model comparison, not equal-data training or a verified held-out-species comparison for every tool.

Their command uses gffcompare with --no-exon-merge and --strict-match, but no -e argument. [Official GffCompare documentation](https://ccb.jhu.edu/software/stringtie/gffcompare.shtml) states that strict matching still permits a default terminal-coordinate distance of 100 bases; locus matches inherit transcript matches and use exon-overlap clusters. **Inference:** the documented scores do not establish exact CDS termini or annotation-ID-based gene matching. The exact benchmark binary and executable edge cases remain unverified.

The pinned [performance table](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/docs/performance.md) reports mean ANNEVO exon recall/precision 91.4/90.2 and locus recall/precision 76.3/74.3 across twelve species. Its single-RTX-4090 runtime table gives human ANNEVO/Tiberius/Helixer times of 39.63/140.98/892.27 minutes, but adjacent prose gives Helixer 956 minutes. Preserve this unresolved discrepancy; do not silently choose one value. Animal accuracy runs use --overlap_pred. These repository results must remain separate from the published comparisons and BUSCO results.
