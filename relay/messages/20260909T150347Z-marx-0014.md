## The cutter now offers the isoform choice instead of waiting for one

Section 6.3 of `docs/data-sources.md` had said the training target could be
one representative isoform per locus "once the benchmark names the
representative". lenin's note says the benchmark will not: its 4.4 matches
whatever a model emits against the best-fitting annotated isoform, so union,
longest CDS and a curated list are all scoreable, and the choice is a
training decision. Agreed, so the decision now lives where it is made.
`cut_windows.py` takes `--isoforms union|longest-cds|representative`:

- `union` (default): every transcript paints, as before.
- `longest-cds`: one transcript per locus, the most CDS bases, ties to the
  longest exonic span, then annotation order; a locus with no coding
  transcript keeps its longest exonic span.
- `representative` with `--representatives FILE` (ids one per line or the
  first column of a TSV, matched with and without a version suffix, so a
  MANE Select export works as is); a locus with no listed transcript falls
  back to `longest-cds` and the sidecar names it.

A locus is the stated gene (UCSC `name2` / `geneName`, Ensembl parent
gene); transcripts with no gene name cluster by overlapping span on one
strand. The sidecar records `isoform_policy`, every dropped isoform with its
locus and reason, and the fallback loci, so a training set built under one
policy cannot be mistaken for another. MANE Select exists for human and
mouse only, so `longest-cds` is the policy that works on every panel
species. Self-test 35 -> 41, identical under `PYTHONHASHSEED` 0, 1 and 42.
Commit 0acda6d on `work/T-human-008-marx`, PR #8.

## What the choice does on a real window

Re-fetched the fly `Adh` 124-way window (hgdownload reset connections again;
the mirror rotation got through in 16 requests, 4.6 MB) and cut it as one
example under both policies with `apiMel4` dropped. The window holds 12
RefSeq transcripts at three loci. All six `Adh` isoforms share one 771-base
CDS and all three `Adhr` isoforms share one 819-base CDS; they differ only
in 5' UTR exons, so `longest-cds` decides them on the tie rule
(`NM_001032098.2`, `NM_058147.4`) and keeps `NM_001169504.2` for the
enclosing `osp`.

| labels (CDS / UTR / intron) | union | longest-cds |
|---|---:|---:|
| bases | 1,590 / 664 / 2,096 | 1,590 / 490 / 2,270 |

174 UTR bases become intron, one boundary mark of a dropped first exon
disappears, and no CDS, strand or frame value changes. That is the general
shape of the cost in RefSeq: alternative UTR exons are far more common
than alternative CDS, so the isoform policy mostly decides what a model is
told about UTRs, which the transcript-level score does not look at. For
T-human-011: if the design trains on `longest-cds`, the transcript column
lenin describes is bounded by how often the longest-CDS isoform is the one
the reference's other isoforms differ from only in UTR, which on this
window is always.
