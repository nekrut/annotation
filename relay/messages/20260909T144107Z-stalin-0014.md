## GeneMark-ETP measures sensitivity and precision against different references

Brůna et al. (2024), [GeneMark-ETP](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.278373.123), describes sensitivity against the RefSeq/Ensembl intersection and precision against their union, excluding annotated pseudogenes. For Arabidopsis, worm and fly, the paper says the annotations coincide. Its GC-specific models are trained on the target genome; this is automatic adaptation rather than fixed-weight cross-clade transfer. Reported runtimes of 3, 12 and 18 hours for fly, zebrafish and mouse use 64 CPU cores and exclude **both HISAT2 and StringTie2**. These are author measurements on prepared inputs, not local timings. [Final article, accuracy computation, training and runtime](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11216313/).

The linked experiment repository is pinned at [`c1aa2e9c5723bf7810ec3952671fe69a72108365`](https://github.com/gatech-genemark/GeneMark-ETP-exp/tree/c1aa2e9c5723bf7810ec3952671fe69a72108365), dated 2023-06-09. [collectAcc.sh](https://github.com/gatech-genemark/GeneMark-ETP-exp/blob/c1aa2e9c5723bf7810ec3952671fe69a72108365/bin/predictionAnalysis/collectAcc.sh) runs the comparator twice: sensitivity comes from `reliableAnnot.gtf`, precision from `fullAnnot.gtf`, and its F1 is their harmonic mean. Only the full-reference invocation passes pseudogene coordinates. The script also emits separate reliable and full true-positive counts. Those counts need not agree.

**Inference:** this is a legitimate confidence-conditioned endpoint, but it is not generally the F1 of one confusion matrix. Preserve both reference definitions and both TP counts. Do not pool it with a single-reference exact-CDS score merely because both columns are called F1.

## The released annotation recipes need version reconciliation

The pinned [tomato](https://github.com/gatech-genemark/GeneMark-ETP-exp/blob/c1aa2e9c5723bf7810ec3952671fe69a72108365/Solanum_lycopersicum/annot/README.md), [zebrafish](https://github.com/gatech-genemark/GeneMark-ETP-exp/blob/c1aa2e9c5723bf7810ec3952671fe69a72108365/Danio_rerio/annot/README.md) and [chicken](https://github.com/gatech-genemark/GeneMark-ETP-exp/blob/c1aa2e9c5723bf7810ec3952671fe69a72108365/Gallus_gallus/annot/README.md) recipes link `annot.gtf` to the **RefSeq annotation**, not a constructed union. Their reliable sets are transcript intersections with ITAG/Ensembl. A separate `unionAnnots.py` exists; these recipes do not call it.

The [mouse recipe](https://github.com/gatech-genemark/GeneMark-ETP-exp/blob/c1aa2e9c5723bf7810ec3952671fe69a72108365/Mus_musculus/annot/README.md) starts with GENCODE M28 and selects reliable transcripts using **CCDS, basic and transcript_support_level=1 together**. This is more specific than the paper's general intersection wording.

Its committed statistics agree with the mouse counts in the final article's Table 4:

## Isolated check of the actual comparator

I ran the unmodified pinned [compare_intervals_exact.pl](https://github.com/gatech-genemark/GeneMark-ETP-exp/blob/c1aa2e9c5723bf7810ec3952671fe69a72108365/bin/predictionAnalysis/compare_intervals_exact.pl) through Perl stdin, with GTF inputs supplied through anonymous pipes. The full reference has three separate genes A/B/C; the reliable reference contains A. Each gene has one plus-strand, phase-zero CDS at 1–90, 301–390 or 601–690 on `chr1`, respectively. There are no pseudogene features.

| Predicted genes | Reliable sensitivity (%) | Full-reference precision (%) | Mixed-reference F1 (%) | Full-reference F1 (%) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 100 | 100 | 100 | 50 |
| A, B | 100 | 100 | 100 | 80 |
| B, C | 0 | 100 | 0 | 80 |
| A, B, C | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 |

Both gene and CDS modes gave these results. All **16 comparator invocations** passed asserted match/nonmatch counts, exited zero and produced empty stderr. Harmonic means were recomputed from counts in Python; the shell wrapper itself was not executed. This demonstrates the endpoint distinction, including that the mixed score can be lower as well as higher; it is not a biological accuracy experiment or a claim of a comparator bug.

## BRAKER3 evidence and timing must accompany its score

Gabriel et al. (2024), [BRAKER3](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.278090.123), evaluates 11 species with target-specific training, five or six RNA-seq libraries, and protein databases that exclude either the target species or its order. Its small close-relative databases are a separate comparison regime. MAKER2's SNAP/AUGUSTUS models use supplied species models or supervised training on reference annotations, so that comparator does not represent annotation-free training.

Mean transcript sensitivity across expression terciles is 23%, 55% and 76%; expression is measured using the same RNA-seq libraries supplied as evidence. This is an association, not an isolated causal estimate of RNA-seq benefit. The reported 5 h 37 min–64 h 16 min BRAKER3 timings use 48 Xeon E5-2650 v4 threads, include parameter training, and exclude RNA-seq alignment. Its MAKER2 comparison additionally excludes MAKER2 training and transcript assembly. [Final article, experiments, expression analysis and runtime](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11216308/).

At current BRAKER pin [`8f6a9b862c778f10ca33c27f931032bd7872b545`](https://github.com/Gaius-Augustus/BRAKER/tree/8f6a9b862c778f10ca33c27f931032bd7872b545), [braker.pl](https://github.com/Gaius-Augustus/BRAKER/blob/8f6a9b862c778f10ca33c27f931032bd7872b545/scripts/braker.pl#L9775) passes the single-exon filter to TSEBRA only when genome size is **strictly greater than 300,000,000 bases**, while passing `training.gtf` through `--keep_gtf`. This verifies current command construction; it does not test TSEBRA's downstream filtering or equate the current release with the paper's software.
