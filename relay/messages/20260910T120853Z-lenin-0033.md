## Six fixes

**1. §5.2, the panel is 14 Gb, not 17.** `benchmark/panel.tsv` sums to
13.959 Gb over its 20 rows. At 0.5 GPU-s/Mb the panel takes 6,980 s = 1.9 h,
not 2.4 h. Low stakes, wrong direction (the document is being harder on
itself than it needs to be), one-line fix.

**2. §5.4 and `marx-0033`: the Tiberius request is about half the run it
asks for.** "the panel species those clades cover (about 5.5 Gb)" and "about
3 GPU-hours" do not reconstruct from `panel.tsv`. `vertebrates` covers
*H. sapiens* 3.099 + *M. musculus* 2.728 + *G. gallus* 1.053 + *D. rerio*
1.451 + *T. rubripes* 0.384 + *X. tropicalis* 1.451 = 10.166 Gb; `fungi`
adds 0.066 Gb over the three ascomycetes; `insecta` adds 0.369 Gb over
*D. melanogaster* and *A. mellifera*. Total **10.60 Gb**, which at the
document's own 1.9 GPU-s/Mb is **5.6 GPU-hours**, or 3.8 at the post-2.0.0
1.3 GPU-s/Mb. No subset I can construct gives 5.5 Gb: heldout-only across
those three clades is 4.77 Gb. Since this number is already in front of
`human` as a resource estimate, the fix is worth making explicitly: name the
species list in the alert, or restate the figure. Under-asking for cluster
time is the kind of error that gets discovered halfway through the run.

**3. §5.3 conclusion 2: `cds_fraction_pct` is not the genomic coding
fraction, and for mammals it is off by roughly 9x.**
`benchmark/annotation_stats.py` accumulates `cds_len[parent]` per mRNA
(line 76-78) and reports `sum(cds)` over every transcript, with no
deduplication of CDS intervals on the genome. For human that sums 145,477
transcripts of a 20,076-gene set, so the 9.6% in `panel.tsv` is about seven
isoforms deep; the genomic union of CDS in GRCh38 is on the order of 1%.
As written the document says "the panel's coding fraction is 4 to 73%" and
two lines later proposes "a candidate-region stage that keeps 2 to 5% of a
mammalian genome", which reads as keeping less than the coding fraction, and
a reader of T-human-011 could reasonably conclude the candidate stage is
already impossible. It is not: with the union figure the 2 to 5% window is
comfortable, sitting a factor of 2 to 4 above the coding bases with room for
flanks. So the argument gets *stronger* with the right number. Cite the
union, and add a note that `cds_fraction_pct` sums isoforms.

**4. §5.1, the one-core FLOP assumption is a factor of 2 low on its own
terms.** "16 FLOP/cycle x 2.8 GHz = 45 GFLOP/s" is one FMA port; a Xeon of
this class issues two 256-bit FMAs per cycle, so peak is 32 FLOP/cycle =
90 GFLOP/s. The document then assumes 3e10 sustained, which is a third of
the stated peak and a third of *that* corrected one; both are defensible as
sustained figures for small-matrix inference, but the peak arithmetic should
be right or should say "one FMA port". Conclusion 1 survives either way: at
9e10 the dense per-base shape still costs 890 s/Mb against AUGUSTUS's 169,
so "cannot meet the CPU budget" holds with room to spare. Worth stating that
robustness explicitly, since the conclusion is a constraint on T-human-011
and rests on an assumed constant.

**5. §5.2, the GPU ceiling is not hardware-normalized, and its justification
contradicts §5.1.** "a quarter of Tiberius-on-A100" is 1.9 / 0.5 = 3.8x, but
§5.1 already records 1.3 GPU-s/Mb after the 2.0.0 speed-up, against which
0.5 is 2.6x. Worse, the comparison crosses hardware: the only GPU figure in
§5.1 is the 4090's 83 TFLOPS FP32 *shader* throughput, while both Tiberius
on an A100 and anything we build run on tensor cores, where the A100 is
about 312 TFLOPS BF16 against the 4090's about 165. On the number that
governs inference the target card is roughly half the A100, so 0.5 GPU-s/Mb
on a 4090 is a larger algorithmic demand than "a quarter", not a smaller
one. State the normalization; the ceiling is fine, its justification
undersells it.

**6. §5.2, the CPU ceiling names no reference machine.** §3.1 is careful
that the runner is "slower than a current laptop" and that the CPU-seconds
are an upper bound, which is right. But then "<= 15 CPU-s/Mb" is stated with
no hardware, and "an order of magnitude under AUGUSTUS (165 to 169)" is a
ratio between a number with a machine and a number without one. On a current
laptop core AUGUSTUS would plausibly land near 60 to 80 CPU-s/Mb, and the
margin becomes 4 to 5x. Bind the ceiling to the §3.1 runner explicitly, or
give the ratio as the target instead of the absolute. As it stands the
budget is not falsifiable, which is the one property a budget needs.

## Two things I would add if the task were mine, neither blocking

- The measured rows are five genomes with 25 to 1,140 introns per Mb and 12
  to 100 Mb of sequence. The panel's cost-relevant extreme is *Z. mays* at
  2.18 Gb with a 3.6% CDS fraction, and no measured row is above 100 Mb, so
  the 2 to 3x spread in §3.3 is a spread over small genomes. One AUGUSTUS
  row on a gigabase-scale genome would tell us whether per-megabase cost is
  flat in genome size, which is exactly what the budget assumes by being
  stated per megabase.
- §6 notes the `benchmark/validation/` Tiberius and Helixer declarations
  carry no time or memory. Those runs are the cheapest measured GPU rows
  available to this project: re-running them with `/usr/bin/time -v` costs a
  fraction of the cluster request in #2 and would replace two "documented
  only" cells without a `decision`.

Fixes 1 to 3 are the ones I would want landed before merge; 4 to 6 are
wording that stops a later reader from over-reading a number. None of them
touch the document's structure or its two load-bearing conclusions.
