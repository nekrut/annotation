## Runtime and resource scope

**Finding 1, recommended addition.** Section 6.1 already contains the
term `(1.648/2.8)*c` for the host decoder, and the text says a
CPU-hosted decoder "can dominate GPU end-to-end wall time", but the
magnitude is never stated next to the 0.5 GPU-s/Mb target. From the
proposal's own inputs: at c = 3 cycles per candidate the decoder alone
is 1.766 s per physical Mb (both strands), 3.5 times the whole GPU
budget; at c = 10 it is 5.886 s/Mb, 11.8 times. So on the GPU rows
(B-gated-GPU, B-full-GPU, and the matched A control) the 0.5 s/Mb target
is reachable only if the decoder runs at least four (c = 3) or twelve
(c = 10) independent (sequence, strand) recurrences in parallel on host
cores, or on the device. Parallelism is available: mouse has 116 such
units and its longest one (chromosome 1, about 344 s at c = 3) is well
under the 1,364 s genome budget; yeast has 32 units totalling 21.3 s
against a 6.0 s budget, so it too needs at least four cores. I
recommend one sentence in section 6.1 and in milestone 2 stating that the
GPU regimes assume a multi-core or device decoder, with the core count
recorded as part of the measured configuration. This is a reporting
requirement, not a design change; the sequential-strand schedule and the
chromosome-state decoder are otherwise consistent with the 8 GB targets.

**Finding 2, supporting arithmetic the Phase 4 charter will need.** The
proposal "requests no cluster allocation" but gives no training-cost
figure. Using its own forward counts times three for backward, over the
full train panel (ten species, 8,262.6 Mb from `benchmark/panel.tsv`):

| Training arm | PFLOP per epoch over the whole train panel, both strands | At the assumed 1e13 FLOP/s | On one core at 3e10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 5.74 | 574 s | 53 h |
| B, K=8, f=0.05 (frozen A, residual only; upper bound since A's forward is still paid) | 8.11 | 811 s | 75 h |
| B, K=8, f=1 (full-tile GPU arm) | 53.2 | 1.5 h | 490 h |

Even a hundred epochs of the full-tile arm is about six GPU-days and
the stratified sampling plan in section 2 will use far less than the
whole panel per epoch, so training fits the charter's one consumer GPU
and the "no cluster" statement holds. Two omissions carry over from
inference: the CRF forward-backward on the host (the same c-cycle term,
about 0.9 CPU-s/Mb per pass at c = 3) and alignment I/O for B. I suggest
the coordinator write a training budget of that order into the Phase 4
charter rather than leaving it open.

**CPU regimes.** The separation into A-CPU, B-capped-CPU, B-gated-GPU and
B-full-GPU is the right handling of the section 4.3 result. The oracle
tables make the consequence explicit: at alpha = 0.05 the capped CPU
regime serves 10.4% of yeast's, 19.3% of worm's and 34.3% of fly's
annotated CDS tiles even with perfect ranking, so on gene-dense genomes
B-capped-CPU will be close to A and any cross-clade accuracy claim for the
comparative channel has to come from the GPU rows. The proposal says this
in section 4.2; I only ask that the eventual result tables keep the
regime label on every row, as the text requires.

## Support admission

The rule is well posed: the seed score is A's own emission contrast, the
dilation and 384-base grid are fixed, the tile rank is deterministic with
a coordinate tie-break, the 12-base lookahead and history match the
dilation radius, and the carried-credit bound `384*sum(q)/(2N) <= alpha`
holds by construction. The acceptance criteria (99% CDS bases, 99% of
clipped 12-base splice flanks, 95% whole chains, macro-averaged over train
development species, before the cap) are stated as targets, not results,
and the failure branch (gate fails, keep A plus the full-tile GPU arm) is
declared. The remaining risk is the one the proposal names: false seeds
on held-out clades with different composition can spend the credit before
real genes arrive, and the required counters (earned / spent / forfeited,
raw versus post-cap recall) are the right instrumentation. No change
requested.

## Metadata and label contract

Section 3.6 is consistent with the GFF3 phase definition: phase h is the
number of bases to skip to the next codon start, so the emitted-CDS count
modulo three before that row is `(-h) % 3`, the per-row check `(-p) % 3`
follows, and only the initial prefix identity is unknown. The 20-base gap
rule, the rejection of overlapping rows (ribosomal slippage), the
full-span conflict mask, the exception/transl_except exclusion, and the
separation of auxiliary boundary targets from chain targets are
internally consistent with sections 3.1 and 4.4, and the audit counts
behind 4.4 reproduced exactly on all four train species. Two remarks,
neither blocking:

- The conservatively excluded classes that no ordinary-ORF grammar can
  emit (selenocysteine `aa:Sec`, stop-codon readthrough `aa:Other`,
  ribosomal slippage) will be scored as misses on held-out genomes under
  the benchmark. Their held-out count is a fixed ceiling for all three
  candidates and should be reported once with the Phase 4 results; that
  is a report, not a cleaning of held-out labels.
- The exception-only tags an ordinary grammar could in principle admit
  after a FASTA check (nonconsensus splice site, non-AUG initiator,
  alternative start codon) are at most 52 fly and 15 mouse chains per
  marx-0047, so the "exclude and report" policy costs little; a later
  admission arm is correctly left out of the first comparison.
