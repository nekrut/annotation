**For the review, benchmark and later design: a 20-base cutoff is not below every genuine spliceosomal intron.** The primary Stentor literature provides a counterexample within the charter's nuclear-eukaryote scope.

[Slabodnick et al., 2017, DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2016.12.057](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2016.12.057), [author-hosted open paper, Figure 3 and associated results](https://vcp.upf.edu/papers/wm-stentor.pdf), reports 15–16-nucleotide introns in the macronuclear genome of *Stentor coeruleus*. The article describes Sanger confirmation and RNA-seq evidence of splicing. Its 9,325 predicted introns are not a claim that every prediction received experimental confirmation; I have not recovered the validation-subset denominator. This is a nuclear example, not a nucleomorph or organelle exception.

[Nuadthaisong et al., 2022, DOI 10.3390/ijms231810973](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms231810973), results §2.1 and methods §3.1, analyzes an annotation obtained from StentorDB on 2020-04-22: **8,173 introns of length 15 and 633 of length 16**, totaling **8,806**. Their reported percentages, 92.81% and 7.19%, agree with those counts after rounding. These are annotation-derived counts, not a new experimental validation of every intron. Keep this dataset separate from the 2017 paper's count and percentages; I have not reconciled the annotation versions. Inference: a filter retaining only lengths at least 20 would exclude all 8,806 entries in this reported set. This is arithmetic on published counts, not a genome download or benchmark run.

**Length alone does not identify the annotation event.** [NCBI's GFF3 documentation, annotation accommodations and ribosomal-slippage sections](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/docs/v2/reference-docs/file-formats/annotation-files/about-ncbi-gff3/#additional-details) distinguishes two relevant cases. Putative assembly errors can cause artificial gaps or overlaps between exon/CDS rows. For ribosomal slippage, a single exon's CDS can be split with a gap or overlap while the corresponding mRNA exon remains unsplit; the CDS carries `exception=ribosomal slippage`. Therefore neither every CDS gap nor every very short gap is a splice junction, and a small gap is not by itself proof of programmed frameshifting. These are documented representation semantics, not a reclassification of the reported yeast, worm or predictor records.

My recommendation for T-human-007/T-human-008 and the later design is to preserve transcript-exon structure and exception provenance before reducing annotations to CDS chains. Localize exceptional events: a feature-level exception should not erase that transcript's genuine junctions. Where the record cannot resolve the event, retain an explicit unresolved-short-gap category and report its prediction/reference counts separately. State any scoring floor as a dataset policy with exclusions, and retain a short-intron challenge stratum. A decoder's length support and a scorer's denominator both need checking against that stratum. I have not changed either owner's files, rescored the existing panel, or proposed that every short predictor gap be accepted as an intron.
