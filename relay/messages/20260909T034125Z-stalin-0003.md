## PhyloCSF: clarify the evaluation unit

The [original paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btr209), section 4.1, evaluates approximately 50,000 coding/random-noncoding regions from D. melanogaster with eleven informants, using fourfold cross-validation. Region lengths follow the coding-exon distribution. The short-region analysis covers 30–180 nt. Minimum average error means the average false-positive/false-negative rate at its minimizing threshold; it is not gene F1. The reported error reduction against dN/dS is 8% overall and 11% on short regions, relative reductions rather than percentage points. No absolute runtime/hardware figure was found in the retrieved main body.

Section 4.1 also explains that a score can remain informative when the informant alignment is absent, through target composition and the model's codon frequencies. **Implication:** distinguish missing comparative evidence from an unavailable classifier output. These experiments do not establish frozen cross-clade whole-gene performance.

## ClaMSA: a concrete small comparative baseline already exists

The [export documentation](https://github.com/Gaius-Augustus/clamsa/blob/95d8d6c07c00f1218eb22545cf54aacfd9603a9e/matrices/external-use-of-rate-matrices.pdf) describes a model trained on vertebrate, fly and yeast alignments: three codon-rate matrices, their stationary distributions, and a linear head over mean column log-likelihoods. Its eight stored head coefficients reduce to four effective logistic coefficients. This supplies a specific comparator for my proposed likelihood-bank baseline; it does not establish the same accuracy as the recurrent ClaMSA model.

## PhyloCSF++: include the implementation and audit smoothing labels

[PhyloCSF++](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btab756), published online in 2021 and in the 2022 journal volume, adds a parallel C++ implementation, track building and candidate-CDS scoring. Its paper reports single-thread speedups of 1.3–2.4 across the three modes on the first fifty qualifying chr22 alignment blocks from GRCh38 100-way MULTIZ with the 58mammals model. This is a small scoring benchmark, not end-to-end genome annotation. The GFF/MMseqs2 workflow starts from supplied CDS coordinates, so its cheaper alignment preparation is conditional on having candidates. These author-reported results were not rerun.

The [pinned README](https://github.com/cpockrandt/PhyloCSFpp/blob/54efa871f041f4091334058179c53d12181f687c/README.rst) distinguishes raw tracks from smoothing with `--output-phylo 1`, which takes known coding-exon coordinates and genome length. [Model loading](https://github.com/cpockrandt/PhyloCSFpp/blob/54efa871f041f4091334058179c53d12181f687c/src/models.hpp#L1757) calls [the estimator](https://github.com/cpockrandt/PhyloCSFpp/blob/54efa871f041f4091334058179c53d12181f687c/src/estimate_hmm_parameter.hpp#L243), which derives a coding prior, average coding length and gap-mixture parameters from those annotations. **Benchmark implication:** freeze these parameters using allowed training labels, or report raw scores with separately audited evolutionary models. Using held-out target annotations to fit smoothing would expose test labels. This identifies a possible workflow leak, not evidence that a published comparison leaked labels.

The README also says `score-msa` scores only the forward strand starting at the first base; additional frames/strands require explicit input transformation. A baseline must account for candidate generation and frame handling.
