## MARS results require a version and a selection split

Flicek and Brent (2006), [Using several pair-wise informant sequences for de novo prediction of alternatively spliced transcripts](https://doi.org/10.1186/gb-2006-7-s1-s8), describes a TWINSCAN extension that combines predicted coding transcripts by CDS overlap. Its revised method uses separate mouse, rat, dog, opossum, chicken and frog informants; the workshop submission instead combines mouse with each other informant inside the conservation model. These are distinct prediction procedures. [Open full text, algorithm description](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1810557/).

The conservation parameters were trained separately for the six informants using 3,072 human RefSeq transcripts from 2,477 loci across 112 genomic fragments. The 13 ENCODE training regions were used to select informant membership and set size. The MARS paper reports across all 44 regions, whereas the main EGASP assessment reports the 31 withheld regions. **Inference:** the MARS paper's tables include informant-selection exposure and are not the official blind-set scores. The membership of the separate RefSeq training set was not recovered this tick, so its overlap with ENCODE remains unresolved. [MARS Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1810557/); [EGASP assessment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1810551/).

## Gene scores and transcript scores move in opposite directions

Percentages below are authors' reported sensitivity/precision; the papers call precision "specificity." They cover coding structures in the human ENCODE regions, with the incomplete-reference qualification below. No cross-species target-transfer claim follows from using several informants for one human target. [MARS Tables 1 and 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1810557/).

| Prediction set | Exon sensitivity | Exon precision | Transcript sensitivity | Transcript precision | Gene sensitivity | Gene precision |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Workshop submission, rescored across all regions | 69.3 | 65.8 | 18.2 | 17.8 | 38.0 | 28.3 |
| Revised MARS, all transcripts | 74.4 | 45.1 | 19.4 | 10.4 | 40.6 | 33.0 |
| Identical transcript supported by multiple informants | 43.0 | 71.6 | 15.1 | 29.6 | 32.0 | 35.0 |
| Identical transcript supported by multiple mammalian informants | 40.0 | 77.1 | 14.8 | 33.7 | 31.3 | 37.7 |

The revised version's gene precision increases by **4.7 percentage points**, while transcript precision falls by **7.4 points** and exon precision by **20.7 points**; these are arithmetic differences from Table 1. MARS reports 1,873 unique coding transcripts against 975 reference transcripts, a calculated ratio of **1.921**. This is prediction multiplicity, not recovery of additional validated isoforms. [MARS Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1810557/).

**Design implication:** agreement across informants is a plausible confidence feature, but selecting only consensus chains trades recall for precision. A future benchmark should measure this tradeoff at the transcript level while also measuring the best recovered transcript per locus. Gains at the latter endpoint cannot by themselves justify emitting extra alternatives. Informant count and identity also need to be kept out of held-out selection, even when the underlying predictor parameters are fixed.

## EGASP treats incomplete references differently from complete ones

The main EGASP paper defines a correct coding transcript through its translation endpoints and coding splice sites. A gene passes when at least one transcript matches. For incomplete references, however, a prediction consistent with the annotated portion can pass even with an additional exon; complete reference coding transcripts permit no additional exons. Consequently, describing all EGASP transcript scores as exact complete-chain identity would erase a material scoring rule. This is documented evaluation behavior; the historical evaluator was not executed here. [Guigo et al. (2006), evaluation definitions and Figure 4, DOI 10.1186/gb-2006-7-s1-s2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1810551/).

EGASP's experimental validation also selected candidates using predictor reliability before testing them. Its tested exon pairs were therefore not a random sample of all extra predictions. **Inference:** the selected validation yield should not be substituted for whole-genome prediction precision. [EGASP experimental validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1810551/).
