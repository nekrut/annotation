## 4.3 reproduces exactly

Every number in both 4.3 tables matches to the last digit: scored
sequences and bp, retained transcripts (6,002 / 28,590 / 30,746 / 97,324),
distinct chains (6,002 / 28,590 / 22,438 / 68,828), plus and minus tiles,
oracle f (0.451476 / 0.242737 / 0.125079 / 0.020037), the K=8 conditional
CPU-s/Mb (36.528 / 23.207 / 15.698 / 8.994), the yeast physical and

worm, 4 s fly, 25 s mouse, so the audit is cheap enough to rerun on every
draft revision.

## Two sensitivities the draft may want to state

**1. The 12-base boundary flank of 4.2 is not where the cost is.** Dilating
every retained CDS block by 12 real bases each side before tiling moves the
oracle f by about 1 to 4% relative: 0.451476 to 0.456010 yeast, 0.242737 to
0.247732 worm, 0.125079 to 0.127057 fly, 0.020037 to 0.020765 mouse
(36.8 / 23.5 / 15.8 / 9.0 CPU-s/Mb at K=8). So "before boundary flanks" in
4.3 can be read as a small correction; the real unknowns remain
false-positive candidates and alignment coverage.

**2. Under the 4.2 per-group quota, the non-redistribution rule, not
alpha, is the binding constraint on mouse.** I applied the 4.2 rule
literally to the oracle tile set, with perfect ranking assumed: groups of
256 oriented tiles, `q = floor(0.05*n/384)` slots per group (12 for a full
group), unused slots not carried across groups or strands. The fraction of
annotated-CDS tiles the quota can serve, even if the seed rule ranked them
perfectly:

| Train species | Oracle CDS tiles | Served under 4.2 per-group quota | Served if the same slots pooled per sequence and strand | Groups holding CDS tiles / exceeding 12 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| S. cerevisiae | 28,385 | 10.4% | 10.4% | 260 / 260 |
| C. elegans | 126,770 | 19.2% | 19.3% | 2,041 / 2,020 |
| D. melanogaster | 91,376 | 29.3% | 36.0% | 2,454 / 2,001 |
| M. musculus | 284,713 | 59.2% | 100% | 20,457 / 8,287 |

Mouse's oracle demand (f = 0.020) is well under alpha = 0.05 globally, yet
the per-group rule serves only 59% of its annotated-CDS tiles (an allocated
f of 0.0119 out of the permitted 0.0469), because CDS tiles cluster:
8,287 of the 20,457 non-empty groups hold more than 12 CDS tiles, and the
27,000-odd empty groups' slots are forfeited. Pooling the identical slot
count per (sequence, strand) would serve every mouse CDS tile at the same
alpha. For yeast and worm the two rules coincide, because nearly every group
is over quota anyway (median 60 CDS tiles per non-empty group, the maximum
my histogram bins), so on gene-dense genomes no redistribution rule helps
and the draft's regime table stands. The gene-sparse case, the one B's
comparative channel is meant for on CPU, is where the buffering choice
decides whether B sees most or all of the coding tiles. A bounded
alternative that keeps the 98,304-base buffer would be a per-sequence
running credit (slots accrue with real bases scanned, spend up to the credit
in the current group); it preserves the global bound `384*sum(q)/(2N) <=
alpha` and the streaming property, and it only changes behaviour where
demand is bursty.

These served fractions are tile-level oracle diagnostics with the same
caveats as 4.3: no seed scores, no alignment filtering, no held-out data,
no timings.
