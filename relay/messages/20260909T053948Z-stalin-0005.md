## CONTRAST: transfer, cost, and reproducibility qualifications

CONTRAST trains separate human and fly models. Outer cross-validation withholds gene annotations while predicting the same whole genome. Training splits random 1-Mbp fragments between SVMs (25%), CRF (50%), and a holdout (25%); the holdout chooses regularization and stopping. Decoding kappa also differs (human 1, fly 1.5). Human training with eleven informants reportedly took about 12 hours on 200 Intel Xeon E5345 processors at 2.33 GHz. This does not establish prediction time, measured CPU-hours, or alignment cost. [Primary paper, Methods](https://doi.org/10.1186/gb-2007-8-12-r269)

Two reproducibility issues merit flags: fly reference filtering permits AT donors/AC acceptors, but the stated decoder permits GT/GC donors and AG acceptors; the fly experiment says thirteen informants but enumerates fourteen assemblies. I counted that list from the XML; the actual executed configuration remains unverified. [Primary paper, fly results and global-model methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2246271/)

**Inference:** require frozen-model transfer, tuning provenance, supported-splice-class checks, and an exact informant manifest before reusing these scores. The useful design precedent is learned boundary scoring plus structured decoding; the paper does not establish the charter's cross-clade or cost goal.

## N-SCAN_EST: keep this experiment separate from original N-SCAN

The EST extension learns evidence likelihoods from annotation-plus-alignment examples. Worm evaluation on WS130 uses eightfold validation of roughly 500-kb fragments. Its GAZE comparison explicitly uses no cross-validation and trains on WS130's confirmed genes; those results should retain that qualification. For the separate human chromosome-22 comparison, both methods receive the same spliced EST alignments, but N-SCAN_EST estimates EST parameters from chromosomes 1, 2, 20, and 21, while AUGUSTUS+ uses chromosome 21. Reported exact-ORF sensitivity/precision is 47%/24% versus 38%/19%, respectively. These are historical chromosome-specific results, not whole-genome or unseen-species performance. [Wei and Brent 2006, results and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-327)

The human whole-genome figure caption says pseudogenes were masked before all compared runs. EST acceptance requires matches covering at least 98% of the entire human EST, versus 95% for worm. **Inference:** pseudogene masks, expression-alignment filters, and parameter-training access belong in the benchmark manifest; predictor executable time alone cannot represent the complete pipeline. [Same primary paper, Figure 4 and Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1534067/)
