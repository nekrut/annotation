## Preprint gene F1 is not exact structure accuracy

The [open preprint](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-6402260/v1/3a5653fd-2255-46ca-aaa2-d229aeacebda.pdf?c=1773385959), DOI [10.21203/rs.3.rs-6402260/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-6402260/v1), defines gene success on page 13 using the highest BLASTP segment: bit-score/e-value thresholds of 50/1e-5, genomic-region overlap, and coverage exceeding 70% of both predicted and reference proteins. Only the longest reference transcript is considered. This permits protein-level recovery without exact exon boundaries; do not merge this gene F1 with exact-CDS metrics.

Its methods describe separate models for five clades, BUSCO-informed species selection, and a 5:1 training/validation division. The wording designates species not used in training as candidate tests, without independently resolving validation exclusion here. The supplement's accession-level membership remains necessary. Its 566-species benchmark is not evidence for one frozen model across all eukaryotes. These are preprint observations, not verified final-journal methods.

## Current benchmark contract and version caveats

The pinned [evaluation notes](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/docs/notes.md) describe removing noncoding transcripts, UTRs, and transcripts with invalid boundary codons or intron lengths. Exon evaluation uses the longest reference transcript; locus evaluation permits a matching reference isoform. They give `gffcompare -r REF PRED --no-exon-merge --strict-match`, but no GffCompare version or executable reference-cleaning script. Therefore retain these as documented evaluation rules, not a reproduced endpoint.

The notes assert that ANNEVO test species occur in neither training nor validation and identify unequal exposure for competitors. This is an author assertion, not an independently checked checkpoint manifest. They identify ANNEVO v2.3.0 and Tiberius v2.0.4; the [current README](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/README.md) identifies ANNEVO v2.3.3 and dates its comparison to June 1, 2026. Pin the benchmark's actual checkpoints, commands and code separately from the current branch.

The [performance document](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/docs/performance.md) says animal accuracy runs used `--overlap_pred`. Its single-RTX-4090 human table reports 39.63 minutes for ANNEVO and 892.27 for Helixer, while adjacent prose rounds ANNEVO to 40 minutes but gives Helixer 956. The inconsistency prevents treating the page as one completely reconciled timing record. No new timing was measured.

## Released training code changes the effective reference

The [training README](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/model_training/README.md) calls this an adapted sequence-only v2.3.x pipeline. It requires longest-isoform preprocessing and external cleaning of premature-stop/short-intron transcripts. These prerequisites must accompany any claimed reproduction.

In [genome_track.py](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/model_training/datamodule/genome_track.py), arrays begin as background zero. The parser takes the first permitted transcript, skips overlapping same-strand entries according to its running coordinate, and skips an mRNA with an internal CDS of length one. It does not return a distinct ignore mask for those skipped labels. The [data loader](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/model_training/datamodule/data_load.py) passes sequence/annotation tensors, and the [training loop](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/model_training/src/seq_step.py) supplies the labels directly to the losses. **Inference:** skipped annotation is not automatically excluded from loss; preprocessing must distinguish untrusted regions from biological background. This does not establish which affected records were present in released-checkpoint training.

The [H5 generator](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/model_training/datamodule/sequence_to_h5.py) traverses records returned by the annotation parser, rather than every FASTA record. Its background sampling parameter is the fraction of available all-zero windows retained, not their desired final dataset fraction. The active training loop consumes the `*_path_2` datasets; the coding-only stage remains commented out. The [checkpoint metric](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/model_training/src/evaluate.py) is one minus mean per-class nucleotide F1 excluding the background class, not decoded-gene F1. Exact species/file membership remains an external input.

## Short and single-exon predictions face a higher score gate

The [decoder launcher](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/decoding.py) sets minimum protein length to 100, converts it to 300 CDS bases, and uses a base CDS score threshold of 0.5. The [active filter](https://github.com/xjtu-omics/ANNEVO/blob/42c920f70f52f96c7c06c665a639ec47d9304fff/src/gene_decoding.py#L243) multiplies that threshold by 1.5 when the CDS is shorter than the length setting **or has one CDS segment**. Thus the length option is not a hard protein-length cutoff, and a long single-exon candidate also needs a score of 0.75.

I re-fetched and SHA-256-checked the relevant source, extracted the original threshold expression via AST, and applied its reject-if-below comparison to synthetic scalar inputs:

| CDS bases | CDS segments | Score | Threshold | Retained by score gate |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 299 | 2 | 0.70 | 0.75 | no |
| 300 | 2 | 0.70 | 0.50 | yes |
| 600 | 1 | 0.70 | 0.75 | no |
| 600 | 1 | 0.75 | 0.75 | yes |
| 300 | 2 | 0.50 | 0.50 | yes |
| 300 | 2 | 0.49 | 0.50 | no |

A separate isolated execution of the original `define_transcript_label` used CDS intervals [0,6), [10,11), [15,21) on an initially zero-labelled sequence. It returned unchanged background labels and a warning, confirming the internal-one-base-CDS skip. All seven expected outcomes passed. These checks omit annotation parsing, tensor losses, candidate detection, Viterbi decoding and neural inference; they do not reproduce genome-level accuracy.

**Benchmark/design implication:** preserve raw and filtered reference denominators; stratify candidate recall and final precision by CDS length, exon count and excluded-annotation class. Report nucleotide checkpoint selection separately from decoded structure evaluation.
