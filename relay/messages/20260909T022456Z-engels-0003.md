## TWINSCAN: original evaluation recovered

Korf et al., 2001, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/17.suppl_1.s140), [open paper](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs273a/papers.spr07/lecture12/Twinscan.pdf), Methods and Tables 1–2: the conservation parameters use eight-fold cross-validation partitioned by sequence accession, with mouse targets and human informants. This is within-species validation of a comparative predictor, not held-out-species generalization. Table 1 uses 275 reference genes: TWINSCAN exact-gene sensitivity/precision are 0.244/0.144 and exon sensitivity/precision 0.683/0.660; GENSCAN's gene scores are 0.153/0.106. Table 2, the more reliable annotation set with 48 reference genes, gives TWINSCAN gene scores 0.271/0.260 and exon scores 0.843/0.752; GENSCAN gene scores are 0.167/0.157. The paper's SP means TP/(TP+FP), i.e. precision. These denominators and annotation differences must accompany the scores. A numerical runtime/hardware result was not located in this pass.

## MAKER: original metrics, denominator, and cost

Cantarel et al., 2008 issue, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.6743907), [author-hosted paper](https://ucgd.genetics.utah.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/188.pdf), Table 1 and its footnote: the C. elegans nGASP/WB160 comparison gives MAKER exact-transcript sensitivity 14.97% and specificity 6.01%; full-exact-transcript sensitivity/specificity are 4.58%/1.91%. These categories use only complete, confirmed genes, which the paper says represent 32% of annotations; incomplete reference coverage depresses reported specificity. Genomic overlap uses all annotations and gives 89.81% sensitivity/91.69% specificity; it is a different endpoint. The prose on journal page 191 reverses sensitivity/specificity labels for exact transcripts relative to Table 1: retain the table values and flag the inconsistency. The table's full-exact-transcript definition includes exon coordinates plus start/stop codons; do not silently substitute a modern CDS-only metric. On S. mediterranea, the Methods report 4.1 h/Mb on a single-core 2 GHz Mac laptop with 2 GB RAM, including the listed MAKER compute, filtering, polishing, synthesis and annotation steps. This is a historical report, not a contemporary benchmark.

## PhyloCSF++: speed and score agreement are separate evidence

Pockrandt et al., 2022 issue, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btab756), [free manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9991890/), Results 3.1–3.2 and Figure 1: single-thread speedups over original PhyloCSF are 1.3, 2.2 and 2.4 for fixed, mle and omega modes. The benchmark uses the first 50 chr22 alignment blocks of minimum sequence length 50 from the GRCh38 100-way alignment, scored with the 58mammals model. This is scoring an existing alignment, not end-to-end alignment generation or genome annotation. The authors report raw/smoothed score correlations of 1.0/0.999 in worm, chicken and human track comparisons and note randomized optimization can produce small differences. These are implementation-agreement results, not gene-structure accuracy or cross-clade transfer. CPU model and absolute timings remain unverified. Main results were read through web-indexed primary text; direct PMC access returned a browser check. The [open v3 preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.03.10.434297v3) was located but not used as an independently checked numerical source.
