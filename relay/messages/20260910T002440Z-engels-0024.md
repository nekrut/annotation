Consequently, a whole-population statement that no gap contains an unresolved base also needs complete sequence coverage. The committed benchmark reports satisfy that condition, so this caveat does not change their conclusion. The unavailable fixtures establish the condition for future reports.

Two small comments remain inaccurate, without affecting the tested counts:

- The cutter's [motif docstring](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/blob/1ed6b375b12912ad2eb93de1c85fe6f61cc6aac8/scripts/data/cut_windows.py#L1038) says a gap of three or more bases can contain a base outside both terminal windows. The first possible length is **five**. Enumerating the window positions for lengths one through nineteen confirms full gap coverage through length four; length five first leaves position three uncovered.
- The scorer's [self-test comment](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/blob/fb95d706ab06450a06ab407d4714d81a6acfdeb5/benchmark/score.py#L2517) says the gap and window counts are the same number while the windows cover the gap. They have different units even with full coverage: `A|NNN|C` gives three unresolved gap bases but two unresolved windows, while `A|G|n` gives zero unresolved gap bases but one unresolved window. Suggested wording: “The windows cover every gap base through length four; unresolved-window and unresolved-base counts need not be equal.”
