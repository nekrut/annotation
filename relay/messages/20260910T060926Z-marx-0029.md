## What is on `work/T-human-009-marx` (branch only, no PR)

`docs/cost-baseline.md`, a complete first draft of the deliverable, plus
`docs/cost-baseline/` with the timing script and the run declarations.

1. **Measured AUGUSTUS rows, cost and accuracy from the same run.** On this
   runner (4-core Xeon 2.80 GHz, 15 GB, no GPU, Ubuntu `augustus
   3.5.0+dfsg-4build5`), one process per sequence, four in parallel, GNU
   `time -v` per process, scored with `benchmark/score.py`:

   | genome | Mb | wall s | CPU user s | **CPU-s/Mb** | peak RSS | nucleotide / locus / transcript F1 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | *S. pombe* (marx-0026, 1 core) | 12.57 | 2,076 | 2,073 | **165** | 403 MB | 0.95466 / 0.92316 / 0.70235 |
   | *S. cerevisiae* | 12.16 | 209 | 698 | **57** | 233 MB | 0.95839 / 0.91850 / 0.78060 |
   | *P. falciparum* | 23.29 | 622 | 2,101 | **90** | 988 MB | 0.88362 / 0.87984 / 0.42754 |

   The *S. cerevisiae* scores equal `benchmark/validation/augustus-Saccharomyces_cerevisiae.json`
   (T-human-007's bioconda run on a 24-core box) to five decimals in every
   field, so the apt build is the same predictor and the timings are
   comparable across the two machines. Two findings from the third genome: the same GHMM costs 57 to 165 CPU-s/Mb across three genomes of similar size, tracking intron density, so any per-Mb budget is a ceiling over the panel, not a constant; and peak memory tracks composition, not length, since one 3.3 Mb *P. falciparum* chromosome at 19.5% GC needs 0.97 GB where the whole *S. pombe* genome needs 0.40. Also, `pfalciparum` sets `stopCodonExcludedFromCDS false` while the yeast sets say `true`, so the scorer flag differs per species; it warns loudly either way.

2. **Primary sources re-read, not just the reviews.** Fetched the current
   READMEs of EGAPx, Tiberius, Helixer, BRAKER and GeneMark-ETP from
   `raw.githubusercontent.com`. EGAPx's own figures: fly 144 Mb, 71 CPU-h,
   3 h wall; chicken 1.1 Gb, 425 CPU-h, 5.5 h wall; 32-CPU, 256 GB minimum
   machine; that is 1,400 to 1,800 CPU-s/Mb. GeneMark-ETP's licence is
   stated in its README: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, which closes the "unstated
   licence" item from T-human-002. Tiberius 2.0.0 claims a 30% runtime
   reduction and now has a web server. Helixer states 8 to 11 GB GPU as the
   floor.

3. **Every documented cost figure in the five reviews, tabulated with its
   source and normalized per Mb where the source states a genome size**
   (BRAKER3 ≤ 4,100 to 8,200 CPU-s/Mb, GeneMark-ETP ≤ 1,500 to 4,800,
   Tiberius 1.9 GPU-s/Mb, Helixer about 10, ANNEVO 0.37 on a 4090). Two
   cross-checks: the 2003 AUGUSTUS paper's 6 min per 1.6 Mb on a 2.4 GHz
   PC (225 s/Mb) and the Tiberius paper's 2 h 25 min per mammal on 48
   threads (≤ 139 s/Mb) bracket my 165; and where reviews disagree, the
   T-human-012 figure is the unsourced outlier every time.

4. **A draft target budget with the arithmetic that forces a design
   constraint.** At 2 M parameters, one token per base and 20 aligned taxa,
   a transformer forward pass is 8 × 10^13 FLOP/Mb: about 2,700 s/Mb on one
   core (16x slower than AUGUSTUS) and 8 s/Mb on an RTX 4090 at a realistic
   12% of its 83 TFLOPS. The proposed ceilings (≤ 15 CPU-s/Mb on one core,
   ≤ 0.5 GPU-s/Mb on a consumer GPU, ≤ 8 GB either way, ≤ 5 M parameters)
   are only reachable if the network runs on candidate support (a few
   percent of a mammalian genome, found by a cheap scan of the KA/KS or
   GHMM-emission class) with a codon-level token axis, and if the tree
   enters as coordinates rather than tokens. That is a constraint for
   T-human-011, derived from sourced numbers, and it is in the draft as
   §5.3 with every assumption labelled.

5. **Not measurable here, with reasons:** Helixer (Docker image only; this
   runner has a Docker client but no daemon), Tiberius (Python ≥ 3.12, GPU),
   EGAPx (below its stated minimum machine). The draft carries a cluster

   one 32-CPU/256 GB node) and Tiberius on the panel (about 3 GPU-h), to be
