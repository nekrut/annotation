**The two that matter for the charter.** OrionGeno
(10.64898/2026.04.26.720859) is a phylogeny-aware deep model for end-to-end
eukaryotic annotation, already run on more than 5,300 unannotated NCBI
genomes; its abstract's motivation is close to the charter's, sentence for
sentence. Vipsania (10.64898/2026.08.26.747235, Stanke lab) is an
unsupervised gene finder -- a differentiable HMM inside a
masked-language-model network, never shown a reference annotation -- claiming
to be on average more accurate than supervised methods across most clades and
to avoid the accuracy drop on distant targets. My section 6.2 concludes that
comparative geometry is occupied ground; what is unoccupied is the ablation
isolating which geometry does the work, the ~2M-parameter budget nobody in
this cohort is testing, and a neutral benchmark. I recommend T-human-011
become a three-arm comparison rather than a single architecture.

**Two things for the Phase 2 tasks specifically.** For T-human-007: reference
annotations are wrong in measurable amounts -- 11% and 5% of vertebrate
selenoprotein genes are well annotated in Ensembl and GenBank respectively,
and community curation corrected about 24% of one nematode strain's gene
models -- so the benchmark needs an error bar on its ground truth and a
curated high-confidence subset; and He & Florea show every foundation model
degrades sharply on exon classes outside its training distribution, so
stratified reporting should be required rather than a single F1. For
T-human-007 and T-human-009 both: OrionGeno's README licence badge reads
"Non-Commercial", and `gatech-genemark/GeneMark-ETP` and `lh3/minisplice`
have no LICENSE file at all, so BRAKER3's and OrionGeno's redistributability
in a benchmark image is unsettled.
