## EGAPx conditionally retrains its HMM

Implementation observations below use the existing inventory revision [`f9a7392b6f60d0d24f28db5c4eb69b0e1984616b`](https://github.com/ncbi/egapx/tree/f9a7392b6f60d0d24f28db5c4eb69b0e1984616b), rather than assuming that the current code matches the older RefSeq article.

In [`ui/egapx.py`](https://github.com/ncbi/egapx/blob/f9a7392b6f60d0d24f28db5c4eb69b0e1984616b/ui/egapx.py#L1328), absent an explicit HMM, the wrapper selects parameters using taxonomy and defaults `train_hmm` to the inverse of `good_match`. The matching routine accepts a complete reference-lineage match, a shared genus, or a shared family for mammals. Providing an HMM defaults training off; an explicit `train_hmm` takes precedence. These are conditional adaptation rules, not a universal frozen predictor.

The [active Gnomon workflow](https://github.com/ncbi/egapx/blob/f9a7392b6f60d0d24f28db5c4eb69b0e1984616b/nf/subworkflows/ncbi/gnomon/main.nf#L104) routes the enabled branch through [four sequential training iterations](https://github.com/ncbi/egapx/blob/f9a7392b6f60d0d24f28db5c4eb69b0e1984616b/nf/subworkflows/ncbi/gnomon-training-iteration/gnomon_training_iterations/main.nf#L24). Each [iteration](https://github.com/ncbi/egapx/blob/f9a7392b6f60d0d24f28db5c4eb69b0e1984616b/nf/subworkflows/ncbi/gnomon-training-iteration/utilities.nf#L24) chains evidence, predicts models and trains HMM parameters from those models on the target genome. The resulting parameters feed the next iteration and final prediction. **Inference:** record the chosen HMM, effective training flag and training time in a baseline declaration. Taxonomic distance can change computational work as well as evidence quality. This was a call-path inspection, not an executed Nextflow run.

## The default intron limit depends on taxonomy and genome size

The [wrapper's `get_max_intron`](https://github.com/ncbi/egapx/blob/f9a7392b6f60d0d24f28db5c4eb69b0e1984616b/ui/egapx.py#L2194) returns a base limit L and genome-size threshold T, in bases:

| Taxonomic branch | L | T |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Viridiplantae | 300,000 | 3,000,000,000 |
| Vertebrates | 1,200,000 | 2,000,000,000 |
| Other | 600,000 | 500,000,000 |

For genome length G below T, [genome setup](https://github.com/ncbi/egapx/blob/f9a7392b6f60d0d24f28db5c4eb69b0e1984616b/nf/subworkflows/ncbi/setup/main.nf#L129) computes `((L*G//T + 99999)//100000)*100000`, using integer arithmetic; otherwise it retains L. Direct arithmetic checks yield 100,000 for a 150-Mb plant, 700,000 for a 1.1-Gb vertebrate, and 200,000 for a 144-Mb organism in the other branch. These are illustrative formula evaluations, not measurements on downloaded genomes. Supplying `max_intron` sets the scaling threshold to zero in the wrapper.

The [top-level workflow](https://github.com/ncbi/egapx/blob/f9a7392b6f60d0d24f28db5c4eb69b0e1984616b/nf/subworkflows/ncbi/main.nf#L110) passes the effective value to protein alignment, both RNA-seq branches and Gnomon. **Inference:** audit the effective limit and intron-length-stratified recall, rather than recording only the nominal taxonomic limit. This inspection does not establish every downstream executable's exclusion semantics or an empirical recall ceiling.

## Documented cost and agreement are different endpoints

The pinned [README FAQ](https://github.com/ncbi/egapx/blob/f9a7392b6f60d0d24f28db5c4eb69b0e1984616b/README.md#faq) reports these AWS Batch examples, using a mix of 8/16/32-CPU instances with 64/128/256 GB RAM:

| Genome | Supplied RNA-seq | CPU hours | Wall hours |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Fly, 144 Mb | One short-read run; 9.7 G bases | 71 | 3 |
| Chicken, 1.1 Gb | Ten short-read and ten long-read runs; 36.7 G and 4.1 G bases | 425 | 5.5 |

These are maintainer-reported pipeline examples; no peak-memory measurement, stage breakdown or matched accuracy endpoint accompanies them there. The prerequisite hardware listing is not a measured minimum.

The same FAQ reports EGAP/EGAPx BUSCO differences within 0.5% and roughly 75–80% of genes having a matching CDS. It does not specify the comparison panel or directional denominator there. This is inter-pipeline agreement, not independent gene sensitivity. The README's Gnomon report separately distinguishes exact splice support from support within five bases; neither measures independent truth when the supporting evidence also constructed the model. ## RefSeq provenance and evidence failure modes

The [RefSeq 2025 article](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae1038) describes EGAPx as a preliminary public release. Its reported mean BUSCO completeness of 97.3% concerns EGAP annotations, assessed in protein mode on the longest protein per gene. It is not an EGAPx exact-gene benchmark. Selection for the animal/plant RefSeq collection favors quality assemblies and generally requires transcriptomic evidence, so these annotations do not constitute an evidence-poor random genome sample.

The same article identifies chimeric STAR alignments spanning adjacent paralogs as a cause of erroneous readthrough models, and describes a compartment-based correction also applied to long-read alignments. **Inference:** fusion/split errors and evidence provenance belong alongside boundary accuracy in the benchmark; RNA support alone does not resolve paralog ambiguity.

NCBI's [pipeline process description](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/refseq/annotation_euk/process/) says known same-species RefSeq transcripts can directly determine annotation, while other accepted models originate from Gnomon. It distinguishes NM_/NP_ and XM_/XP_ provenance and permits some protein-supported coding models with corrections relative to the genome. **Inference:** separate curated and predicted reference subsets, retain evidence-source overlap declarations, and handle corrected/pseudogene models explicitly. A RefSeq-versus-Gnomon comparison can partly measure agreement with related model-generation machinery. Accession prefixes alone do not certify independent experimental truth.
