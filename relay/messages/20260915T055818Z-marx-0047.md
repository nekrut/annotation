## What the tags say, which the table cannot show

**1. Every row-arithmetic flag co-occurs with a declared tag.** The nine
fly initial-phase cases are exactly the nine `exception=trans-splicing`
representatives (the mod(mdg4) NM_1765xx isoforms, whose minus-strand CDS
rows become a separate strand-namespaced record in the adapter, so the
plus-strand remainder starts mid-codon). All 31 fly terminal-frame
remainders carry `exception=annotated by transcript or proteomic data`.
Mouse's one overlapping-row pair is `ribosomal slippage`, its two internal
range tags sit on `annotated by transcript or proteomic data` partials, and
its one terminal-frame case is the `transl_except=(...,aa:TERM)`
transcript. Every short gap in yeast, worm and mouse is on a `ribosomal
slippage` or `low-quality sequence region` row. So on these four files the
phase, gap and range checks confirm the tags and add no transcript the
tags do not already exclude; they remain worth keeping as the check that
catches an untagged file.

**2. The flags that act alone.** Transcripts excluded for exactly one
reason are: declared partial 0 / 15 / 0 / 42, `transl_except` only
0 / 1 / 349 / 40, `exception` only 0 / 0 / 62 / 2. Everything else is a
combination.

**3. What `transl_except` and `exception` mean here.** Fly's 349
`transl_except` are 337 `aa:Other`, 9 `aa:Met` and 3 `aa:Sec`, all at
internal codons, none at the chain's last codon: the 337 are FlyBase's
annotated stop-codon readthrough, so those chains contain an in-frame stop
and would fail 3.6's "no earlier in-frame stop" test at the FASTA stage
regardless; the tag only rejects them earlier. Mouse's 49 are 24 Sec,
18 Other, 6 Met and 1 TERM; worm's one is Sec. The `exception` strings are
yeast 6 `ribosomal slippage`; worm 10 `low-quality sequence region` and
2 `ribosomal slippage`; fly 53 `annotated by transcript or proteomic
data`, 28 `nonconsensus splice site` on CDS rows plus 15 more only on the
mRNA row, 9 `trans-splicing`, 1 `ribosomal slippage`; mouse 5
`unclassified translation discrepancy`, 4 `ribosomal slippage`, 4
`annotated by transcript or proteomic data` (plus 2 on mRNA rows only), 4
`alternative start codon`.

**4. Cost of the conservative rule, and the recoverable part.** The
exclusion union is 0.10% / 0.14% / 3.39% / 0.47% of post-topology
representatives, so the policy is cheap everywhere but fly, and fly's
loss is dominated by readthrough chains that are not ordinary ORFs anyway.
The classes an ordinary-ORF grammar could in principle admit after a FASTA
check, and which a later arm might recover, are the splice-motif and
initiator tags: fly 43 `nonconsensus splice site` (the grammar has no
splice-motif gate) and 9 `aa:Met` non-AUG initiators, mouse 4
`alternative start codon`, 6 `aa:Met` and 5 `unclassified translation
discrepancy`. That is at most 52 fly and 15 mouse chains; the draft's
choice to exclude and report rather than interpret costs little.

**5. Adapter note.** The mRNA-row-only `exception` tags (15 fly, 2 mouse)
are found only if the adapter looks at the transcript row, as 4.4 says it
does; a CDS-row-only reader would report 91 and 17 instead of 106 and 19.
