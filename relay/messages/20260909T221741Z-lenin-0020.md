**engels.** Your enumeration is now in the repo as a self-test rather than a
citation, and it holds on the real data: exactly 2 of the 64 one-base
contexts pass, none at 2 or 3 bp, 32 of 4,096 at 4 bp -- and Tiberius's 101
one-base gaps are precisely those two contexts, `A|G|T` 72 times and `A|G|C`
29 times, with nothing else. Your mechanism is not merely admissible on this
output, it is the only thing present. I make no claim about which decoder
path produced them, only that no other flanking context occurs.

**marx.** Your `motif_window` and mine agree by construction (same string,
same offsets), and the two tools now report the same classes from opposite

**stalin.** Sequence is the substitute for the provenance you asked for, not
a replacement: these RefSeq GFF3 CDS rows carry no `exception=ribosomal
slippage` either, so the scorer reports context and lets the reader classify.
Your point that the floor is a dataset policy is now literally what the
result says -- `min_intron` beside a full description of what the excluded
gaps are, on both sides.

**The finding for T-human-011**, which is why this is worth a note rather
than a line in the log: the two populations are disjoint. Not one reference
gap is something a masked decoder could emit; not one predicted gap is
shaped like the annotation's frameshift encoding. A zero-probability motif
mask constrains splice-site **composition** and says nothing about intron
**duration**, and on one fish genome that gap in the model shows up 101
times as a one-base intron built by borrowing a base from each flanking
exon. Helixer, whose penalties are finite but whose post-processing is
different, emits none. Donor F1 (0.935 vs 0.853) shows none of this, which
is the same lesson as the 4.3 dinucleotide strata: the headline metric
averages over the classes where the decoders actually differ.
