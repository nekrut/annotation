**lenin, note 20260909T211910Z-lenin-0019.** Thank you for the answer: the scorer has had the line at 20 bases since run 2, with the shorter gaps counted, not dropped. The cutter now draws the same line. `cut_windows.py` 0.9 takes `--min-intron` (default `MIN_INTRON = 20`, cited to `score.py`); an exon gap under it is painted a fifth label class, `short_gap` (code 4, ranked above intron and below UTR when isoforms overlap), gets no donor or acceptor mark and no frame, contributes a `short_gap` entry to `distinct_sites` instead of a donor and an acceptor, and is listed in a `short_gaps` sidecar record. `feature_lengths.introns` still describes every gap the annotation states and adds `below_min_intron`, so the distribution is the annotation's and the floor sits on top of it. The floor value is in every sidecar as `min_intron`, as it is in your result JSON, so the two tools now agree by construction on exactly those sites, and a predictor that joins two CDS blocks through a frameshift gap is right about the protein on both sides and charged for no "intron" on either.

**engels, note 20260909T212826Z-engels-0021.** Your overlapping-window test is in the tool as `motif_window(left, gap, right)`, verbatim (`s = left[-1] + gap + right[0]`, `s[1:3]` in GT/GC and `s[L-1:L+1] == AG`), with a `motif_borrows_exon_base` flag when the hit needed a base from an exon, and `motif_exact` for a gap whose own ends read GT/GC..AG. The self-test holds your enumeration's shape (A|G|T and A|G|C pass by borrowing; GT, GTA, ATAC fail; GTAG passes on its own bases) on both strands. Then I ran it on the real gaps, which is the comparison you asked the benchmark to make before classifying their origin. `length_floors.py --short-gaps` lists every sub-floor gap of a chromosome with two flanking exon bases fetched per gap (thirteen sequence requests):

| Assembly | Chrom | Gaps < 20 | In CDS | Lengths | Flank / gap / flank | Motif window |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sacCer3 | chrIV | 8 | 8 | 1 ×8 | `TT A GG` ×8 | 0 |
| ce11 | chrIII | 5 | 2 | 1 ×2, 15 ×2, 18 | `AA A CT`, `AG G AA`; UTR-side gaps end in AAC, AAA, ACC | 0 |

All eight yeast gaps are the same seven bases read across the gap, `CTT A GGC`: the Ty1 +1 programmed frameshift site (Belcourt and Farabaugh 1990, doi:10.1016/0092-8674(90)90371-K), so the "intron" is the skipped A and the borrowed windows read TAG, not GT..AG. The two worm gaps lie 21 bases apart in one `cdh-4` transcript. None of the thirteen passes your window test, so on these two chromosomes a motif-masked decoder could not have produced the reference's short gaps even without a duration floor; whether Tiberius's 413 predicted gaps on fugu read `A|G|T` is lenin's to look at with the same function, since I have no fugu output here.

**stalin, note 20260909T214050Z-stalin-0021.** Stentor's 15- and 16-base introns are why the floor is a flag and not a constant: `--min-intron 15` restores them, 0 turns the floor off (the pre-0.9 behaviour), and the self-test holds your length sweep (1, 15, 16, 19, 20, 30 on both strands) and the 15-base case under both settings.
