step-3 paragraph) now says "4,096-base seam overlap retaining the 2,048-base
chain-containment guarantee" and cites the `chromosome.tiles` bound with

**Revision step 3, packed back-pointers.** `fast_viterbi.scan_segments`
now returns `(score, PackedBackPointers, final)` instead of the three dense
tensors: `prev` is kept as the two argmax slots of `U` and `E("")` that the
scan already computes (2 B per boundary; every other state has exactly one
predecessor, so the dense column is a constant expanded on traceback),
`exit_r` as nibbles (12 B, valid while `2R < 16`), `entered` as `K R = 72`
bits (9 B) — **23 B per base instead of 120**, packed per tile as the scan
produces them and expanded one row at a time for the traceback.
`viterbi_segments` and `chromosome.predict_sequence(segments=)` use it;
the single-window functions are unchanged. `SeamCarry` now also checks the
store's byte count and bit-for-bit equality of each row's expansion with
the unbroken scan's `prev` (t ≥ 1), `exit_r` and `entered`; 189 tests pass
under 3.11 + torch.

Re-measured yeast chr I under the same one-core protocol (records in

The packing costs nothing measurable in the decode stage (1.57 → 1.60 s,
run-to-run noise). RSS moves only where the pointers were the largest live
tensor (the two-row exact decode); the 19-segment row's peak is the
per-tile operands at `B = 38` (`(12,288, 38, 24)` float64 tensors, ~90 MB
each) and is independent of chromosome length. Where the packing matters
is the metazoan row: at 20 Mb / 19 segments per strand the held state is
now 0.92 GB of pointers + 1.76 GB of float32 emissions (held twice today,
because `viterbi_segments` pads the per-segment emissions into one
`(B, 11, L)` tensor before scanning) + ~0.6 GiB operands + one segment's
dense expansion during traceback, about 3.5 GB against 8 GB, where the
dense store alone was 4.8 GB. Not a metazoan measurement yet.
