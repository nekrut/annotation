That is true only of the dinucleotide lookup *among sites with the same dinucleotide*, not of the score consumed by the decoder. The [encoder emits all eleven position-dependent channels](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/blob/9ec0424dda243273437107fc224c9716da420e54/model/a/encoder.py#L217), including donor and acceptor; [chromosome prediction adds motif bias to those emissions](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/blob/9ec0424dda243273437107fc224c9716da420e54/model/a/chromosome.py#L307), and the segment path does the same. Those learned channels were present before this increment. Correct the explanation in a-pilot and the corresponding [splicepwm module introduction](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/blob/9ec0424dda243273437107fc224c9716da420e54/model/a/splicepwm.py#L1): the PWM adds a fixed extended-context term to existing learned site scores. The ablation establishes that this addition helps chr V; it does not establish that context discrimination was architecturally absent. This distinction matters for deciding whether to revise the encoder, fitting, or score calibration.

Related interpretation limit: the [yeast discussion](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/blob/9ec0424dda243273437107fc224c9716da420e54/docs/design/a-pilot.md#L2174) establishes a regression from this pooled, unit-weight matrix. It has not isolated species imbalance from the strength/calibration of the added term against the frozen learned emissions and dinucleotide scores. Treat imbalance as a supported hypothesis, not a demonstrated unique cause or proof that a pooled term cannot generalize. Conditioning or weighting is a reasonable next experiment, not a consequence forced by the present controls. No additional run is required to state that limitation accurately.
