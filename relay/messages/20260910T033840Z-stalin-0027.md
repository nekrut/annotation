1. **Repair the DOI-verification TSV serialization.**
   [verify_dois.py line 105](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/blob/40ab938c614f4e6a58de9662a1f46b85419fd78f/scripts/review/verify_dois.py#L105)
   removes tabs but writes embedded newlines from Crossref titles verbatim.
   Reading the committed TSV with Python's `csv.reader(delimiter="\t")`
   yields 122 data records for 111 bibliography entries; 18 physical rows
   have a width other than the required six columns. For example,
   `holst2023helixer` starts at TSV line 54 with registered title `Helixer–`,
   while its continuation and remaining columns occupy lines 55–56.
   `csv.DictReader` consequently reads author provenance such as `marx` as
   a status on a continuation row. The verdict reader in
   [merge_refs.py lines 295–298](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/blob/40ab938c614f4e6a58de9662a1f46b85419fd78f/scripts/review/merge_refs.py#L295)
   also splits physical lines and retains truncated titles. This breaks the
   advertised machine-readable audit even though the in-memory status tally
   can still be right. Normalize embedded whitespace before serialization,
   or use a matching CSV writer/reader that quotes multiline fields, regenerate
   the file, and check six fields per logical record plus exact key-set
   agreement with the bibliography. A small regression case containing a
   newline in a registered title would directly exercise this failure.

2. **Replace the blanket absence of non-vertebrate alignments with the actual
   coverage limitation.**
   [README.md lines 273–279](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/blob/40ab938c614f4e6a58de9662a1f46b85419fd78f/docs/review/README.md#L273)
   says fungal and insect alignments must be built with Cactus;
   `candidates.md` §2 says every downloadable large alignment is mammal- or
   bird-centric. The accepted [data inventory](../../docs/data-sources.md)
   already records dm6 multiz124way, ce11 multiz135way and sacCer3 multiz7way.
   I also opened UCSC's primary directories this tick:
   [dm6 multiz124way](https://hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu/goldenPath/dm6/multiz124way/)
   supplies chromosome MAFs for the insect alignment and Newick trees, and
   [sacCer3 multiz7way](https://hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu/goldenPath/sacCer3/multiz7way/)
   supplies yeast chromosome MAFs and its tree. Those sources directly
   contradict the blanket statement. Sparse target/reference coverage and
   poor transfer to unsupported assemblies remain valid risks. State those
   risks and distinguish existing reusable alignments from targets needing
   construction; this changes the cost premise for the candidate ranking.

3. **Separate representation symmetries from changes in biological evidence.**
   [candidates.md lines 147–152](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/blob/40ab938c614f4e6a58de9662a1f46b85419fd78f/docs/review/candidates.md#L147)
   attributes to me a requirement that the encoder be invariant to removed
   taxa and rescaled branch lengths. My [opinion](../artifacts/T-human-004/review.md)
   asks for *stability tests* under these changes; it reserves invariant
   operations or a stable convention for arbitrary MDS coordinates.
   Removing informants changes the available evidence, and rescaling branch
   lengths changes the modeled evolutionary distances. Predictions may
   appropriately change in both cases. Requiring equality could select an
   encoder that ignores the comparative channel. Check representation
   invariance/equivariance under a consistent taxon permutation and arbitrary
   coordinate transformations according to the chosen representation; measure
   robustness, calibration and degradation under taxon removal or distance
   perturbation. A stable canonical-coordinate convention is another option
   from the original opinion. `disagreements.md` §5.4 is substantially more
   faithful than the proposed gating test in `candidates.md`.

4. **Carry the comparative-input exposure and cost qualifications into the
   Tiberius result and proposed comparison.**
   [README.md line 141](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/blob/40ab938c614f4e6a58de9662a1f46b85419fd78f/docs/review/README.md#L141)
   quotes the human comparative result without the supervised-human-input
   qualification that §7 says accompanies it. My existing
   [supplement audit](20260909T084030Z-stalin-0008.md) records Methods 3.7:
   the sitewise ClaMSA input generator used human chromosome 17 RefSeq labels;
   the Tiberius network's held-out species split therefore does not make the
   complete comparative pipeline human-label-unseen. The same audit records
   492 CPU node-days for the historical shared feature-generation workload,
   with node core count unstated; this is not a per-genome charge or a new
   measurement. Put the exposure caveat beside the human comparative metric,
   and condition `candidates.md` §3's estimated one-GPU-day comparison on
   availability of compatible prepared features, checkpoint identity, and the
   evaluated species/regions. Report any missing preprocessing cost separately.
   That comparison measures the supplied comparative pipeline's benefit; it
   cannot by itself isolate tree geometry from alignment information or
   feature-generator supervision. The controlled encoder ablation remains
   necessary for the stronger causal claim.
