**Top five findings.**

1. **Per-species fitting has been dented, not overturned — and the trend is
   visible in the counts.** All five reviews reach this. Helixer ships four
   lineage models, Tiberius six, ANNEVO six, Vipsania seventeen. Twenty-two
   years after Korf's "every genome needs a dedicated gene finder", the field
   has moved from one model per genome to a few models per kingdom. The
   remaining 8% of assemblies outside Tiberius's six clades is precisely the
   tail a species-independent method would be judged on, so a benchmark drawn
   from the 92% cannot measure species independence.

2. **Alignment availability is the top risk, named independently by all five
   reviews.** The comparative design assumes a good alignment with a tree at
   every locus in a novel genome. Every downloadable large alignment is
   mammal- or bird-centric; for the clades where annotation is most needed
   there may be no informant at a useful distance, at which point the cost has
   moved rather than gone and the clade dependence returns as "works where
   relatives have been sequenced". The single-sequence fallback has to be
   designed in from the start, and the degradation curve as informant density
   and divergence fall should be a required experiment.

3. **Whether the tree helps at all is the open question, not a settled
   premise.** Three independent lines say so. CONTRAST discarded the phylogeny
   in 2007 and still gained from more informants (gene Sn 50.8 → 58.6, one to
   eleven) where N-SCAN's explicit phylogenetic model could not. Tiberius ships
   nine configurations of which exactly one uses comparative ClaMSA input and
   it is mammals-only, while all six clades added in 2026 are sequence-only.
   And ClaMSA already trains phylogenetic likelihoods discriminatively, so
   "add a tree to a classifier" is not novel. This does not refute the charter —
   it means the charter's central claim is the thing nobody has measured, which
   is the strongest argument for measuring it.

4. **The comparative-geometry design space is occupied; the measurement is
   not.** OrionGeno is explicitly phylogeny-aware and has been run on >5,300
   genomes; ANNEVO reports 566 species. Neither isolates the contribution of
   phylogenetic context from that of long-range modelling. An ablation
   separating tree-as-metric from tree-as-extra-tokens from no-tree, at a fixed
   parameter budget through one fixed decoder on one benchmark, is cheap and is
   the deliverable nobody else is producing.

5. **The evidence base needs handling with care, in two specific ways.** The
   ground truth is measurably wrong — selenoproteins well annotated for 11% of
   genes in Ensembl and 5% in GenBank; >7,500 gene models (~24% of one
   annotation) corrected by curation of a single nematode strain; 63.8%
   non-canonical or incomplete genes in the *T. nigroviridis* reference BRAKER2
   itself used. And nine DOIs in the merged bibliography resolve to unrelated
   papers or to nothing; they are retained but flagged `DO NOT CITE` with what
   they actually point at, and no claim in the synthesis rests on them.
   Separately, three different repository slugs were called "Helixer", one of
   which is a personal fork with zero stars.
