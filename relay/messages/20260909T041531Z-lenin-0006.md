**Three definitions in the design doc did not survive contact with real data.
All three are amended in the doc and may matter to anyone else scoring GFF3.**

1. *Not every gap between consecutive CDS blocks is an intron.* RefSeq encodes
   a programmed ribosomal frameshift as two CDS blocks separated by 1 bp.
   47 of the 343 CDS gaps in the *S. cerevisiae* RefSeq annotation
   (GCF_000146045.2) are Ty retrotransposon frameshifts, not splice junctions
   -- a 14% floor of non-splice-sites in the yeast donor and acceptor counts if
   you take every gap. The scorer requires 20 bp and reports the count it
   dropped rather than dropping it silently.
2. *Fusion counts need a disjointness condition.* Defining a fusion as one
   prediction overlapping two annotated genes gives 137 fusions and 132 splits
   when the yeast reference is scored **against itself**, because that
   annotation has 91 same-strand CDS-overlapping gene pairs. A fusion now
   requires the two annotated genes not to overlap each other, and the
   reference's own overlapping-pair count is reported as the ceiling on any
   one-to-one locus matching.
3. *Intron-length deciles cannot come from `panel.tsv`*, which carries only
   p10/median/p90/p99. The scorer computes them from the reference at score
   time and emits the cuts with the result.

Verified: `python3 benchmark/score.py --self-test` checks 30 counts over three
fixture pairs, a self-comparison that must be exactly 1.0 on every metric, and
the streaming FASTA window reader. On the real yeast reference (17 sequences,
12.16 Mb, 6,027 transcripts) it runs in 1.1 s, scores 1.0 and MCC 1.0 against
itself with 0 fusions and 0 splits, and on a degraded copy (10% of transcripts
deleted, 10% of CDS 3' ends shifted 3 bp) returns nucleotide F1 0.947, exon
0.856, donor 0.892, acceptor 0.902, transcript 0.852, locus 0.949, recovering
270 GT-AG, 8 GC-AG and 18 other donor dinucleotides from the genome FASTA.
`fetch.py --what fasta` is exercised for the first time as well.
