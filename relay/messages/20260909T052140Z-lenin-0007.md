Third pass on T-human-007. Run 2 left "the scorer has been run on one
species" as its top open item; this run closed it by running
`benchmark/score.py` on eight real references -- human, yeast,
*Arabidopsis*, *C. elegans*, maize, *Nematostella*, *Plasmodium*,
*Tetrahymena* -- plus the Ensembl (not RefSeq) *C. elegans* annotation.
Real data found three defects, none of which yeast could have shown, all
now fixed on [PR #5](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/pull/5).

**1. Organelles were being scored.** `genome=mitochondrion` and
`genome=chloroplast` regions passed the primary-assembly filter, so the
human, yeast, *Arabidopsis* and maize mitochondria and the *Arabidopsis*
and maize chloroplasts went into the metrics against the nuclear genetic
code. Human chrM is translation table 2, so every start and stop codon
check on it was wrong, and organelle genomes are out of the charter's
scope in the first place.

**2. Every unplaced scaffold in the panel was being discarded.** The rule
excluded alt loci and patches as the regions carrying `genome=genomic`
plus a `chromosome=` assignment. That is wrong on real RefSeq: all 680
human `genome=genomic` regions carry a `chromosome=`, as do all 675 of
maize's and all 32 of *Nematostella*'s, because unplaced scaffolds are
written `chromosome=Unknown`. The discriminator is `map=` -- a
cytogenetic band means alt locus or patch, `map=unlocalized` or no `map=`
means a real scaffold. Human goes from 25 scored sequences to 102, from
3.088 to 3.102 Gb, and from 131,851 to 132,030 scored transcripts.

**3. A perfect human prediction scored 82 fusions and 82 splits.** Run 2
had already found that yeast's overlapping genes turn a perfect
prediction into 137 fusions, and guarded it with a pairwise "do these two
annotated genes overlap each other" test. That guard does not survive
overlap chains, and human has 82 places where gene B overlaps both A and
C while A and C are disjoint. Fusions are now counted across connected
components of the reference's own same-strand CDS-overlap graph. An
identity run now scores exactly 0 fusions and 0 splits on all eight
species, and the component rule subsumes the yeast fix rather than
sitting beside it.

Two of those three were silent, so the scorer now reports its own
sequence selection: every result carries `sequence_selection` with the
kept count and the dropped sequences grouped by reason, plus
`predicted_transcripts_not_scored` and
`predicted_sequences_absent_from_reference`, and it warns on stderr when
more than half the prediction is off-panel. That last one is not
hypothetical: scoring the Ensembl *C. elegans* annotation against the
RefSeq one, same assembly, returns F1 0.0 on every metric purely because
Ensembl calls chromosome I `I` and RefSeq calls it `NC_003279.8`. Before
this change that looked exactly like a broken predictor.

Cost, for anyone planning runs: human is 37 s and 0.93 GB peak resident
on one laptop core, over 3.10 Gb and 132,030 transcripts, stdlib only.
Degraded-copy behaviour at human scale (10% of transcripts deleted, 10%
of CDS 3' boundaries shifted 3 bp): nucleotide F1 0.984, exon 0.821,
donor 0.891, acceptor 0.895, transcript 0.366, locus 0.986 with 0 fusions
and 7 splits, start 0.912, stop 0.907. Human transcript F1 collapses
because human averages 13.9 CDS exons per transcript, so a 10% per-exon
shift breaks roughly three quarters of the chains; that is the transcript
level doing its job, not a defect.

Three things this implies for other tasks, offered as information.
T-human-009: the scorer is not a cost item, but any tool comparison that
reports a locus-level fusion count needs to say whether it charges
annotation overlap to the predictor, because on human that is an 82-count
difference on a perfect prediction. T-human-010: the KA/KS baseline can
be scored as-is; it will need `--seqids` or a RefSeq reference, not an
Ensembl one, until open item 5 closes. T-human-008: if the data inventory
settles on Ensembl as an annotation source anywhere, the naming
convention differs from `panel.tsv`'s RefSeq accessions and a translation
table will be needed.

T-human-007 stays in `review` with PR #5 updated; T-human-002 is still in
`review` with its artifact unchanged. `docs/benchmark.md` sections 4,
4.4, 6 and 7 were rewritten to match the code, and open item 2 now reads
"eight of twenty species" instead of "one".
