## Helixer: qualify the draft's exact-transcript wording

The [final paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02939-1), Methods, describes longest-protein transcript selection, UTR removal, and GffCompare 0.12.8. The [published supplement](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41592-025-02939-1/MediaObjects/41592_2025_2939_MOESM1_ESM.pdf), page 11, supplies the command: after removing exon/UTR feature rows, it invokes GffCompare with reference and output arguments, without `--strict-match` or `-e`. The [pinned repository procedure](https://github.com/weberlab-hhu/Helixer/blob/d17bb496b8842542590644b2437798711c0125d5/docs/comparing_annotations.md) agrees.

[GffCompare's official documentation](https://ccb.jhu.edu/software/stringtie/gffcompare.shtml) says ordinary transcript matching accepts identical intron chains despite differing outer boundaries, and significant overlap for single-exon transcripts. Strict mode applies a terminal-distance tolerance, whose default is 100 bases; `-e` controls it. Source help and argument parsing in both [0.12.6](https://github.com/gpertea/gffcompare/blob/96f058181acb376b6da4ff692792cf3c7bb3cb55/gffcompare.cpp) and [0.12.9](https://github.com/gpertea/gffcompare/blob/f7b04b6e6000715db07b5b9278104a6b51890d1b/gffcompare.cpp) show this opt-in behavior. I did not recover or execute the exact 0.12.8 binary.

**Inference/correction:** the documented procedure does not establish exact CDS start/stop agreement. My draft's unqualified “exact primary CDS comparison” wording is too strong. Preserve the published scores as GffCompare transcript scores under the stated preprocessing. This identifies an endpoint issue; it does not recompute scores or show a ranking reversal. Before a shared benchmark uses this scorer, verify shifted termini, single-exon overlap, strand, CDS phase and stop-codon conventions with executable fixtures.

## Original Tiberius: isoform policy and small-model comparator

The [2024 paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae685), sections 2.7 and 3.1.2, selects the longest CDS from both references and predictions for its evidence-based comparisons. Consequently, it evaluates a different output-selection policy from any-reference-isoform locus matching. Section 3.1.1 already reports a parameter-size ablation: increasing from 2 million to 8 million parameters improved gene/exon F1 by 4.9/1.2 percentage points in that mammalian study. This supplies a pre-existing small-model comparator; it does not demonstrate pan-eukaryotic transfer. Section 3.2.3 explicitly describes declining performance outside the mammalian training domain. These are author-reported results, not measurements this tick.

## SegmentNT: distinguish held-out labels from sequence exposure

The [SegmentNT paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02881-2), Methods, holds human chromosomes 20/21 for testing and 22 for validation, and removes test chunks with annotated gene homology to training/validation genes. Its multispecies model is fine-tuned on human plus mouse, chicken, fly, zebrafish and worm; ten animal and five plant species are held out from this supervised stage. The described cross-species orthology filter is against human training/validation genes. It does not by itself establish exclusion of orthologs from every other training species.

The paper produces multilabel nucleotide predictions, including overlapping isoforms, and evaluates them with nucleotide metrics. That does not establish reconstruction of separately phased, valid transcripts. The backbone is pretrained NT-v2, so supervised species holdout also needs a separate pretraining audit.

The [backbone model card](https://huggingface.co/InstaDeepAI/nucleotide-transformer-v2-500m-multi-species/blob/06615c1660c892fc199840c18123f8385b3542a8/README.md) reports a corpus of 850 genomes excluding plants and viruses. I parsed the pinned [original URL manifest](https://huggingface.co/datasets/InstaDeepAI/multi_species_genomes/blob/b7326579fa3528b5896f5f4aa1554ad69ad5f105/original_urls.csv) as newline-separated URLs, then split URL path components. It contains 850 unique URLs: 667 bacteria, 46 fungi, 39 invertebrates, 10 protozoa, 31 mammalian and 57 other vertebrates. Two relevant entries are:

- Rat: `Rattus_norvegicus/GCF_015227675.2_mRatBN7.2`.
- Anemonefish: `Amphiprion_ocellaris/GCF_002776465.1_AmpOce1.0`.

Both taxa are among the paper's supervised held-out species. This is evidence of species exposure in the released sequence corpus, not annotation-label leakage. I have not verified the historical checkpoint's exact training stream or byte-level assembly equivalence to the evaluation assemblies. The documented plant exclusion should also be retained; a blanket claim that all test taxa were pretrained on would be unsupported.
