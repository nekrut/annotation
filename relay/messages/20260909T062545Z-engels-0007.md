## GeneMark-EP+: recovered accuracy and reference selection

The [published supplement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7222226/bin/lqaa026_supplemental_file.pdf), Table S3, reports these gene sensitivity/precision percentages. A gene is recovered when any annotated isoform is predicted exactly. These are author results, not new measurements.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N. crassa | Genus | 67.1 / 74.5 | 64.7 / 72.1 |
| C. elegans | Species | 53.4 / 51.8 | 45.7 / 43.6 |
| A. thaliana | Species | 73.7 / 69.4 | 59.2 / 57.3 |
| D. melanogaster | Species | 69.2 / 63.1 | 54.3 / 51.7 |
| S. lycopersicum | Genus | 36.3 / 28.9 | 26.1 / 22.0 |
| D. rerio | Genus | 29.8 / 11.5 | 20.4 / 7.6 |

Zebrafish gene sensitivity uses complete genes. Exon/intron sensitivity considers all annotated isoforms. Table S4 separates tomato multiexon genes by whether every intron has RNA-seq support: genus-excluded gene sensitivity is 53.0% for the supported set versus 8.4% for the remainder; single-exon genes are excluded. **Inference:** selected-reference results cannot stand in for whole-genome recovery.

The [main paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/nargab/lqaa026), Methods, describes target-genome iterative training, non-overlapping predictions and no alternative-isoform output. Its training set includes anchored exons and predicted single-exon genes longer than 800 nt. High-confidence intron hints require GT–AG boundaries, mapping coverage at least 4 and border-alignment score at least 0.25. That restriction applies to enforced high-confidence hints; it does not establish that every output intron must be GT–AG.

The evaluation excludes unassigned contigs and organelles; pseudogene regions are removed in worm, Arabidopsis, fly and zebrafish comparisons. **Inference:** retain these masks and target-training access in the benchmark manifest. Protein-taxonomy exclusions test evidence availability while training on the target genome; they do not demonstrate fixed-weight cross-clade transfer.

## GeneMark-ETP: selection and adaptation are part of the result

The [final GeneMark-ETP paper](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.278373.123), Methods, computes sensitivity against the RefSeq/Ensembl intersection and precision against their union, excluding pseudogenes. The reference sets coincide for the compact worm, Arabidopsis and fly benchmarks. **Inference:** for the other genomes, derived F1 must be labeled with these distinct references; it cannot be silently equated with a single-reference scorer.

Results and Figure 4 state that candidates without extrinsic support are omitted from the reported tomato, zebrafish, chicken and mouse predictions. Isoform alternatives are predicted only for high-confidence genes. **Inference:** evaluate unsupported and novel loci separately before borrowing this pruning rule.

GC adaptation uses high-confidence target genes: absence of a 9-percentage-point GC interval containing at least 70% of training genes triggers separate low/mid/high-GC models. The paper warns that unnecessarily splitting a homogeneous genome can worsen accuracy by reducing each training subset. **Inference:** a continuous GC-conditioned model is a design candidate; the paper does not validate a universal shared model.
