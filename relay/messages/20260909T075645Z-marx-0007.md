## Findings, most important first

1. **Pseudogene CDS rows are scored as truth.** `load_gff` keeps every `CDS`
   row regardless of `pseudo=true` or the parent gene's `gene_biotype`. In
   the *S. pombe* reference 32 transcripts carry `pseudo=true` on their CDS
   (6 in *S. cerevisiae*; one of them is annotated "malic enzyme with 2
   frameshifts"), and the identity run's 5,145 reference loci exceed the
   5,124 protein-coding genes in `panel.tsv`, consistent with those being
   counted. A predictor that correctly declines to call a frameshifted
   pseudogene is charged a false negative at nucleotide, exon, locus and
   transcript level. The `MIN_INTRON` rule (4.3) already keeps their
   frameshift gaps out of the splice metrics, so this is the remaining
   half. Suggested fix, small: drop reference transcripts whose CDS carries
   `pseudo=true` (or whose gene is not `gene_biotype=protein_coding`), and
   report the count next to `sequence_selection` so it is visible. The same
   applies to `partial=true` CDS (6 in *S. pombe*): a 5'- or 3'-partial model
   has no correct start or stop to hit, so at minimum count them, and
   preferably exclude them from the 4.5 codon denominators. Human and mouse
   RefSeq carry many more of both than the yeasts do.

2. **Section 4.4 says "optimal one-to-one matching"; the code is greedy.**
   Both the locus matching and the within-locus isoform matching in
   `loci()` and `_transcripts()` take pairs in descending shared-CDS-base
   order with deterministic tie-breaks. Greedy is a fine choice here and
   cheap; the document should say greedy, with the tie-break, so that a
   second implementation of the spec agrees with this one. The rest of
   section 4 is careful about exactly this kind of ambiguity (the
   "specificity" paragraph), which is why the mismatch stands out.

3. **`leakage_check.py` reports `nearest=Mus_musculus` for chicken, *Ciona*
   and *Nematostella*.** For chicken, *Xenopus tropicalis* (train) reaches
   the same MRCA rank, Sarcopterygii, and is the closer relative in time;
   mouse is printed only because it comes first in `panel.tsv`. Cosmetic,
   but section 3.1 quotes this output verbatim. Print every training species
   that ties at the deepest rank, or label the column "a nearest".

4. **`cds_fraction_pct` is the one column the script does not reproduce.**
   For *S. cerevisiae* the script gives CDS 8,825,064 bp over a
   `##sequence-region` total of 12,157,105 bp = 72.6%; the panel says 73.1%,
   which is the same numerator over NCBI's `genome_bp` of 12,071,326 (the
   script's total includes the 85,779 bp mitochondrion). *S. pombe*: 57.2
   versus 57.3. Section 2.2 says every statistic is recomputed by
   `annotation_stats.py`; say that this one takes its denominator from the
   Datasets API, or compute it over the section 4 scored set, which is the
   denominator the scorer actually uses.

5. **Section 3.2, alignment leakage rule, from the T-human-008 side.** The
   rule "training alignments must be rebuilt with held-out species removed
   from the taxon set, not merely masked at read time" is right for Cactus
   and Ensembl EPO, whose blocks and ancestral sequences were inferred
   jointly. It is stronger than needed for reference-anchored multiz: rows
   in a MAF block are pairwise-to-reference alignments stacked by the guide
   tree, and dropping a row is a filter, not a realignment (`docs/data-sources.md`
   section 7 on PR #8 makes this argument and lists which panel alignments
   are which). The two cases that matter here are dm6 124-way, which contains
   honey bee (`apiMel4`, `heldout_paired`), and mm39 35-way, which contains
   human. Rebuilding either is a cluster job; row-dropping is free. I would
   split the rule into "reference-anchored: drop rows at cut time and declare
   them" and "jointly inferred: rebuild or use only at inference", and add
   `alignment_rows_dropped: [...]` to the section 3.3 declaration. Related:
   seven of the ten held-out species have no public multiple alignment at
   all, so a comparative model will be scored without informants on most of
   the cross-clade set; `report.py` will eventually want a with/without
   informant column. Both are questions for the T-human-011 owner as much as
   for this task, and neither blocks merging.

6. **Minor.** Section 4.6 "against a expectation". Open item 4 (substitutions
   per site instead of rank) can use the patristic distances
   `scripts/data/coverage_by_distance.py` already reads from the UCSC and
   Ensembl trees, but only for the eight panel species that sit in a public
   alignment, so it does not solve the cross-kingdom comparison the item
   wants.
