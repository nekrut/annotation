(sections 4.2, 4.3 and 6.1). This is a bounded design tick; T-human-011 remains

## What changed

- Specified a sequence-score seed rule using A, a bounded 98,304-base group
  buffer with lookahead, and a fixed per-group tile quota. Its illustrative
  5% cap allows 12 of 256 tiles per full group and bounds mean allocated
  support, including terminal groups. The cap is a work bound; a Phase 4
  pilot must establish any feasible positive CPU quota. Extra positions
  retain A's predictions. Separate A-CPU, B-capped-CPU, B-gated-GPU and
  B-full-GPU rows prevent a capped result from standing in for unrestricted B.
- Audited cached panel GFFs for four train species, checking each panel md5
  and using the accepted scorer's sequence/transcript filters. Selecting
  every tile intersecting retained annotated CDS gives mean oriented
  allocation **0.451476 yeast, 0.242737 worm, 0.125079 fly, 0.020037 mouse**.
  These are annotation-only oracle requirements, before boundary flanks or
  false-positive candidates; they are not detector performance. At K=8 they
  imply **36.528, 23.207, 15.698 and 8.994 conditional CPU-s/Mb**, respectively,
  from counted encoder work. Reproduction code, denominators and source
  hashes are in section 4.3.
- Corrected the conversion between a genomic CDS union and the draft's
  fraction: f uses **2N** for both orientations. A physical 72.35% yeast
  union cannot be directly substituted as f. The direct strand/tile audit
  still supports marx's warning that unrestricted CDS refinement exceeds
  the CPU budget on gene-dense examples.
- Counted the maximum non-overlapping distinct reference CDS chains under
  a single path per strand, with codon/gap restrictions ignored. These are
  optimistic topology bounds, **not benchmark transcript-F1 ceilings**:
  unused reference isoforms at matched loci do not count as false negatives
  in the accepted scorer. The actual longest-CDS/conflict-mask label audit
  remains to be done.
- Recomputed explicit stem-efficiency, decoder-cycle and scratch-bandwidth
  scenarios from marx-0044. They show why a 5% CPU quota is not yet a runtime
  promise. Rechecked the public axomeme URL (404), consistent with lenin-0040;
  the implementation-access gap remains part of the eventual decision.
