## What checks out

- All seven TSV rows reproduce from the embedded formula (rerun here, six
  decimals). A's formula matches its section 3.5 inventory: four blocks of
  `12*96^2` (attention projections plus expansion-four MLP), attention over
  16 offsets, kernel-nine stem and depthwise blocks on `2N*4104/3072`
  positions.
- The two divisors, 3e10 CPU FLOP/s and 1e13 GPU FLOP/s, are exactly the
  cost baseline's section 5.3 rates, used the way that section uses them
  (conditional arithmetic, not timing), and the 15 CPU-s/Mb and 0.5 GPU-s/Mb
  ceilings are quoted correctly. The CPU ceiling is user CPU time summed over
  all processes per Mb (section 3, `measured.tsv` convention), so a
  one-core conditional figure is the right thing to compare against it.

## Three uncounted items priced with the baseline's own conventions

These are arithmetic under stated assumptions, in the same sense as the
draft's numbers, and the owner may use or discard them.

1. **Decoder.** Section 3.3 gives at most 1.648 billion transition candidates
   per Mb (delayed entry, both strands, forward plus replay). On the
   section 3.1 runner's 2.8 GHz core that is 0.59 CPU-s/Mb at one cycle per
   candidate, 1.8 at three cycles, 5.9 at ten. A scalar max-plus update with
   a gather over 165 active scores is unlikely to run at one cycle per
   candidate; three to ten is the plausible band. So the decoder alone
   plausibly takes a quarter to all of the 4.09 s that B at K=8, f=0.05
   leaves for omitted work. AUGUSTUS's measured 25 to 169 CPU-s/Mb
   (section 3) is a GHMM Viterbi with order-4 or 5 content sensors and is
   not a clean comparator, but it is the only measured decoder cost on that
   runner.
2. **Stem at realistic efficiency.** The 12.5685 GFLOP/Mb of kernel-nine and
   depthwise convolutions on 16 channels is memory-bound, not GEMM-bound.
   Section 5.1 already warns that small-matrix inference reaches well under
   the assumed rate. At a tenth of it the stem costs 4.2 CPU-s/Mb instead of
   0.42; at a thirtieth, 12.6. The wide 96-channel blocks on about 223 k
   tokens per Mb are large enough GEMMs that 3e10 is defensible for them.
   This is the one place where the 3e10 assumption is likely wrong in the
   draft's disfavour, and it is A's cost as much as B's.
3. **Scratch.** Spooling 176 MB/Mb (section 3.3) costs 0.35 s/Mb at
   500 MB/s (laptop SATA SSD) and 0.09 at 2 GB/s (NVMe). This one fits.

## The point I would make about the ceiling's anchor

The CPU ceiling's portable form is 1/11 of AUGUSTUS's CPU-s/Mb on
*S. pombe* on the same machine (section 5.2), quoted on that genome
deliberately because AUGUSTUS's own cost varies 7x across the panel. The
draft's maximum allocated fraction under the counted work, 0.1141 at K=8,
is set against CDS-union fractions of 72.4% for *S. cerevisiae*
(`docs/cost-baseline/cds_union.tsv`; *S. pombe* is of the same order, about
60%, not in that table). By the draft's own formula B at K=8 and f=0.6 to
0.72 costs 46 to 54 conditional CPU-s/Mb, three to four times the ceiling,
before the decoder. So under the CPU budget the "compute cap disables
comparative refinement" regime that section 1 names is not an edge case;
it is the normal regime on exactly the genome the ceiling is quoted on, and
on every gene-dense genome in the panel. Two consequences the draft may
want to state outright:

- On CPU, the A-versus-B comparison on gene-dense genomes is an A-only
  comparison. B's comparative channel is testable on CPU only where support
  is a few percent (mammals, maize), which is also where alignments exist
  and where AUGUSTUS is cheapest per Mb (25 CPU-s/Mb on human chr21), so
  the margin over the GHMM there is 1.6x, not 11x (section 5.2).
- On GPU, B at f=1, K=8 is 0.21 conditional GPU-s/Mb, inside the 0.5
  ceiling with room, so B on gene-dense genomes is a GPU regime under the
  budget. Declaring "B-dense: GPU only" and "B-sparse: CPU or GPU" as two
  input regimes, each with its own benchmark row, would make the budget
  claim falsifiable per regime instead of per candidate.

None of this changes the recommendation to develop B with A as control; it
sharpens what "meets the budget" can mean for each.
