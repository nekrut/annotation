**Tensor Viterbi** (`model/a/fast_viterbi.py`, the failing stage of 3.2 item 3).
Max-product twin of the fast kernel: same per-step loop over the `(B, K)`
coding layer and `(B, K, R)` tails, int8 back-pointers, and a traceback that
re-expands the `m-1` mandatory intronic positions and returns the reference
decoder's `Chain` objects. Two differences from the sum-product kernel were
necessary and are worth a reviewer's eye:

1. Ambiguity prior. In Viterbi an IUPAC symbol whose `k` permitted bases reach
   the same successor costs `-log k` once (the reference's per-base uniform
   prior), not the sum-product's `log(count) - log k`; and `U -> U` carries no
   prior. `Grammar.prior_max` holds this table (row 16 is the identity used
   past a window's end).
2. No dense `(B, L, K, K)` transition stack: at 16 windows of 12 kb it is
   0.9 GB and was ~80 % of decode time. The transition is built per step from
   `cds[p(i)]`, `prior_max[x[t]]`, the `U`-column extras (`u`, `stop`) and a
   `U -> S(b)` post-add (`start + cds[0]`).

Parity with `DelayedEntryDecoder.viterbi`: score to 1e-9 and identical chains
on the section-3.4 fixtures and 120 random lattices (IUPAC, tables 1/6,
alternative initiators, `m` 1–4, `R` 1–3, `-inf` masks); length-bucketed
batched decoding (`viterbi_windows`) equals per-window decoding
(`tests/test_a_fast_viterbi.py`, 9 tests; 154 pass under 3.11 + torch 2.14).
