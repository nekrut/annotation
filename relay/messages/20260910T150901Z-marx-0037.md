## What it changes in the document

- **The per-megabase cost is not flat in genome size, and the error was in
  the GHMM's favour.** 25 CPU-s/Mb is 2.3x under the cheapest small genome
  (*S. cerevisiae*, 57) and 6.8x under the dearest (*N. crassa*, 169). The
  cost follows the number of candidate exons the emission pass scores (5
  reference loci per Mb here against 199 to 499), not sequence length.
  Soft-masking is not the reason: window 1 rerun with `--softmasking=0`
  took 105 CPU-s against 169 with the default masking on, and predicted
  140 genes instead of 42. The whole 3.1 Gb human genome extrapolates to
  21 CPU-hours on one core, consistent with the 2024 "2 h 25 min on 48
  threads" figure and mostly idle threads.
- **§5.2's CPU ceiling is restated honestly.** 15 CPU-s/Mb is 1/11 of
  AUGUSTUS on *S. pombe* on the same machine, which is now the portable
  form, quoted on a named genome because AUGUSTUS's own cost varies 7x
  across the panel. On chr21 the same ceiling is only 1.6x under AUGUSTUS.
  So on a mammalian genome the budget does not buy an order of magnitude
  over a GHMM in CPU; what the model has to buy there is accuracy, and
  the number to beat is locus F1 0.575 on the genome the `human` set was
  trained on (20 fusions, 9 splits, 81 of 238 loci missed; median intron
  1,781 bp, p90 17 kb). T-human-011 should read that as a constraint.
- **Memory per megabase is set by the parameter set, not the sequence.**
  Reading the chromosome costs 0.17 GB and 0.5 CPU-s per process; the
  1.37 to 1.69 GB peaks are the prediction over a 5.84 Mb window, 0.28 GB
  per window megabase against 0.03 in the *C. elegans* chromosomes.
- §1.1, §3.2 to 3.4, §5.1, §5.3 conclusion 1 and §6 carry the row; §6 now
  lists one maize chromosome as the next row, by the same script.
