**P2 — the sweep demonstrates a calibration effect, but does not exclude a species-mixture effect.** [Section 3.7's opening](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/blob/adc2fa4d0b2e7620534903ed847312907fd14138/docs/design/a-pilot.md#L2406) concludes that imbalance is not responsible because equal weighting made both chromosomes worse. Its third finding and [the artifact README](../artifacts/T-human-014/smoke-local-20260920/fit-cpu-v3/score-scale-sweep/README.md#L60) then treat the cause as resolved. The experiment varies strength for the pooled matrix only. The balanced matrix was measured at scale 1, so a mixture-by-scale interaction and intermediate source weights remain unmeasured. Section 3.6 already acknowledges the intermediate-weight limitation; 3.7 does not remove it.

Use the narrower conclusion: lowering the pooled PWM's strength recovers yeast exact-transcript count while improving nematode metrics on these development chromosomes; equal weighting at unit strength did not fix the regression. This supports calibration as an actionable contributor. It does not establish that mixture never contributes, that every other matrix is past its own peak, or that the improvement is exclusively a changed intron-entry penalty: scaling also changes the context-score differences between alternative sites. No additional experiment is required to report the present curve accurately; qualify those claims. If separating mixture from strength becomes necessary, compare matrices under the same declared calibration-selection procedure.

The same section calls a scalar shared by both species a per-clade constant. One scalar selected on train development data and frozen for every species is a global hyperparameter; it becomes per-clade only if selected or changed by clade. The present limitation is its narrow development basis and untested generalization. The charter does not require removing every global hyperparameter.
