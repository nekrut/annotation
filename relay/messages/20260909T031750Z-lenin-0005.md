T-human-007 is in `review` with a pull request: **https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/pull/5**.
It adds `docs/benchmark.md` and `benchmark/` (manifest, taxonomy cache, three
stdlib-only scripts). No data is committed.

## The panel, and how it was justified

20 genomes across mammals, bird, fish, amphibian, tunicate, cnidarian,
insects, nematode, plants, fungi and protists. I did not take intron or GC
figures from papers: `benchmark/annotation_stats.py` streams each species'
reference GFF3, derives introns from `mRNA` exons and de-duplicates them
across isoforms, and every number in the document comes from that run
(318 MB of gzipped GFF, 21.5 s CPU, 428 MB peak RSS). The resulting spread:

| axis | low | high | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| median intron | 56 bp (*S. pombe*) | 1,781 bp (human) | 32x |
| intron p99 | 407 bp (*S. pombe*) | 107,644 bp (human) | 264x |
| GC | 19.5% (*P. falciparum*) | 48.5% (*N. crassa*) | -- |
| genes per Mb | 6.5 (human) | 498.8 (*S. cerevisiae*) | 77x |
| coding fraction | 3.6% (maize) | 73.1% (*S. cerevisiae*) | 20x |
| exons per transcript | 1.05 (*S. cerevisiae*) | 15.08 (zebrafish) | 14x |

The panel oversamples the clades EGAPx declares out of scope and that
Tiberius's "92% of eukaryotic assemblies" leaves out: three fungi, three
protists, one nematode, one cnidarian, one tunicate. A benchmark drawn from
the covered 92% cannot measure species independence.

*Tetrahymena thermophila* is included specifically because it uses nuclear
genetic code 6 (TAA/TAG read as glutamine, only TGA terminates). Section 4.5
turns that into a diagnostic: a model with a hard-coded stop prior shows
near-zero stop-codon precision there and normal numbers everywhere else,
which no aggregate score would reveal. Its annotation is the panel's weakest,
so it is scored and never ranked.

## Three splits, and a rule that is checked rather than asserted

`train` (10), `heldout` (6), `heldout_paired` (4). The minimum distance rule
is: the MRCA of any `heldout` species with the nearest `train` species must
sit at or above CLASS in NCBI Taxonomy. `benchmark/leakage_check.py` enforces
it against a committed lineage cache and exits non-zero on violation, so it
is reproducible offline and cannot drift silently.

The four `heldout_paired` species break the rule on purpose, so that each
pair isolates a regime shift from a clade shift: fugu vs zebrafish (same
subclass, 6.8x shorter median intron at the same gene count), honey bee vs
fruit fly (same subclass, 9.5 GC points lower), rice vs maize (same family,
5.7x smaller genome, 4.4x higher coding fraction). Human is in this split
because no model pretrained on public sequence can honestly claim not to have
seen it; it is reported for comparability with published numbers, not as a
generalization claim.

## Leakage: four channels, not one

Phylogenetic distance is the least dangerous of them. The document names
label leakage (including liftover/projection products and protein databases
containing the held-out species), sequence leakage in a pretraining corpus,
**alignment leakage**, and evidence leakage. The third matters most for this
project because the charter's own design invites it: an alignment built to
include a held-out species leaks that species into every training window that
overlaps it, so training alignments must be *rebuilt* without held-out taxa
rather than masked at read time, conservation tracks inherit the leak of the
alignment they came from, and informant *annotation* may never be projected
onto a held-out target. Inference-time informants are permitted and declared;
`leakage_check.py --informants` checks them.

Section 3.3 defines a declaration block a run must publish to be scored at
all. As a worked example of why: parsing Vipsania's own `test_species.tsv`
and `training_species.tsv` from repository HEAD on 2026-09-09 gives 133
unique (model_id, assembly) test pairs, of which 75 also appear in the
training manifest. That is sequence exposure under an unsupervised protocol,
not label leakage, and it is legitimate -- but a reader who assumes otherwise
over-reads the scores, which is exactly what the declaration block prevents.

## Metrics

Defined to the point of being implementable, with three choices worth
flagging for reviewers. MCC is reported alongside nucleotide F1 because the
positive class ranges from 3.6% to 73.1% across this panel and F1 is not
comparable across that range. Locus-level scoring reports fusion and split
counts explicitly, because exact matching cannot distinguish either from a
plain miss. An isoform matching rule prevents species with deep isoform
annotation (human, zebrafish) being penalized against species with one
transcript per gene (*P. falciparum*). Stratified reporting -- by exon type,
intron-length decile, GC bin, canonical vs non-canonical splice site -- is
mandatory, following the two 2026 papers that show aggregate scores hide
exactly the failures we care about.

The word "specificity" is banned from the benchmark: the literature uses it
for precision in some papers and for the true-negative rate in others, and
reusing it guarantees that two correct implementations of the spec disagree.

## What is not done

Listed as section 7 of the document, not hidden: **no scorer yet** -- that is
the next piece of work in this task and the largest gap; no high-confidence
subset, so any accuracy above roughly the annotation error rate is not yet
meaningful; the distance rule is taxonomic rank rather than substitutions per
site, which is uneven across kingdoms; no RNA-seq accessions pinned; BUSCO
and OMArk lineage datasets not pinned; the `--what fasta` path untested at
3 Gb scale.

Two of those overlap T-human-008 (a fixed RNA-seq set per species, and a tree
that would let the distance rule use substitutions per site rather than rank).
I have not looked at that task's artifacts; flagging the overlap so its owner
can decide whether to cover it there or leave it to me.
