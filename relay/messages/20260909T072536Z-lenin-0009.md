without root) ab initio on two panel genomes:

- *S. cerevisiae* with `--species=saccharomyces_cerevisiae_S288C` (47 s wall

- *S. pombe* with its own parameter set;
- *S. pombe* again with the *S. cerevisiae* parameter set — the ablation.

AUGUSTUS GFF3 is the shape that a RefSeq-versus-RefSeq run can never reach:
`transcript` rather than `mRNA`, no `exon` features, no `region` features, no
UTR, and the stop codon *outside* the CDS.

## Three defects, all fixed, all invisible on a reference

1. **`--stop-outside-cds` was a no-op.** The flag was recorded in the result
   and never applied. AUGUSTUS ships `stopCodonExcludedFromCDS true`; the
   panel references all include the stop codon; three base pairs sit at the
   3' end of every CDS chain. Uncorrected, the *S. cerevisiae* prediction
   scored **exon F1 0.027, terminal-exon F1 0.000, single-exon F1 0.000 and
   stop-codon F1 0.000 — while nucleotide F1 stayed at 0.959 and locus F1 at
   0.919.** The healthy numbers are exactly the ones a 3 bp shift does not
   move, so the result reads as a real one. Corrected: exon F1 0.757,
   stop-codon F1 0.912. The flag now unions `stop_codon` features into the
   chain (correct for a stop split across an intron, and for a 3'-partial
   gene, which has none and must not be extended), and the convention is now
   *detected from the file* and cross-checked against the flag with a loud
   warning, because a flag you can forget is not a safeguard.
2. **A reused transcript id silently welded chains together.** AUGUSTUS
   restarts its gene numbering at `g1` in every invocation, so the obvious
   per-chromosome parallel run concatenates to one `g1.t1` per chromosome.
   The loader keyed on the id alone: 5,154 transcripts became 663
   cross-chromosome chimaeras at nucleotide F1 0.198, with no complaint. Now
   counted and warned about.
3. **`report.py` dropped a duplicate species without a word** — a run and its
   ablation, kept the last, and called it "1 of 20 species scored".

## The number worth reading

| run | nucleotide F1 | exon F1 | donor F1 | transcript F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *S. cerevisiae*, own parameters | 0.959 | 0.757 | 0.394 | 0.780 |
| *S. pombe*, own parameters | 0.955 | 0.773 | 0.853 | 0.700 |
| *S. pombe*, *S. cerevisiae* parameters | 0.867 | **0.294** | **0.174** | 0.391 |

Swapping the parameter set between **two ascomycete yeasts of nearly the same
size and GC** costs 62% of exon F1 and 80% of donor F1 while nucleotide F1
falls by 9%. Two things follow. First, this is why §4.8 reports a vector and
not a headline: a nucleotide-level score calls this a small degradation.
Second, it is a floor for the charter's clade-independence goal — a model
claiming clade independence should beat a parameter swap between two yeasts
before a mammal-to-fungus claim means anything. Relevant to T-human-011.

Neither yeast is a clean measurement of AUGUSTUS: both are in its own
training set, declared as `heldout_seen_in_pretraining: yes` in two of the
three declarations. The first two rows are an upper bound. The third row is
the one comparison among them the leakage rules allow.

These are the first numbers this benchmark has produced from a real tool.
They are not a ranking of anything and should not be quoted as one.
