Section 3.7 of `docs/design/a-pilot.md`, PR 38 at
[`adc2fa4`](https://github.com/nekrut/annotation/pull/38). Inference
only, no refit, no held-out species touched.

**The result.** Section 3.5 found that the splice-site PWM produces the
largest accuracy increment in this pilot and simultaneously breaks the
nearly intronless yeast chromosome. Section 3.6 tested the obvious
cause — a 253:1 *C. elegans* to *S. cerevisiae* junction imbalance —
by re-estimating the matrix with equal per-source weight, and that made
both chromosomes worse. This tick sweeps what 3.6 named instead: one
non-negative scalar multiplying every log-odds entry, so the matrix
fixes the *shape* of the junction preference and the scalar sets how
loudly it speaks against the frozen learned emissions and the
intron-entry hazard it competes with. Nine points on the same `best.pt`,
tiling, scorer and pinned pooled matrix (`a964e3e6…`), no mask.

**The yeast failure was loudness, not species mixture.** Chr I is flat
at 63–65 exact transcripts from scale 0 through 0.75 and only then
falls. Its false GT-AG introns, on a chromosome with three real ones,
go 0, 12, 12, 16, 23, 39, 67, 551, 1,133. Nothing about the matrix
changed across these nine runs. A *C. elegans*-dominated matrix is not
by itself what breaks *S. cerevisiae*; a *C. elegans*-dominated matrix
turned up to a volume calibrated on nothing is. That explains why 3.6's
reweighting could not help.

**Every PWM measurement in this document so far was taken past the
peak.** At 0.75 chr V gains 264 exact transcripts over scale 1.0
(830 → 1,094), exon-exact F1 0.51987 → 0.59758, nucleotide F1
0.76614 → 0.80815, locus F1 0.63826 → 0.71351, donor F1 0.640 → 0.728,
acceptor 0.664 → 0.763, transcript F1 0.133 → 0.193, fusions 740 → 576.
It is bought entirely out of intron over-prediction: predicted introns
38,908 → 25,637 against 22,620 reference GT-AG, false GT-AG
20,082 → **8,863**, nucleotide precision 0.643 → 0.723 against
sensitivity 0.948 → 0.916. At full strength the term was not placing
introns better, it was making intron entry too cheap.

**Controls and verdicts.** Scale 0 is byte-identical to `score-final/`
on both chromosomes (chr I `6cd5faab…`, chr V `5f649777…`) and the two
score JSONs differ in exactly one top-level key, the declaration that
states the scale — the scalar reproduces "no PWM" rather than
approximating it. Cost is flat across the whole sweep (chr V
9.08–9.16 CPU-s/Mb, chr I 12.39–12.82), including the scale-2.0 point
that predicts 126,304 false GT-AG introns, so **no positive CPU
allowance for B follows from this section either**, and **A still
misses the accuracy target**: transcript F1 0.193 as `benchmark/score.py`
computes it, the largest increment since 3.5 and not acceptance.

**What it does not establish, and where I would most like the review to
push.** The selection criterion — unweighted mean of exon-exact F1 over
the two development chromosomes, unweighted so the 20.9 Mb nematode
cannot outvote the 0.23 Mb yeast — was fixed before the points above 1.0
were run, but it is still a criterion I chose, and the two chromosomes
disagree on the argmax: **0.5 is the only swept scale at which yeast is
not worse than no PWM at all** (0.59193 vs 0.58182), and 0.75 costs
yeast 0.033 exon-exact F1 to gain chr V 0.078. Only the term's strength
was swept, not the intron-entry hazard it competes with, so the peak is
conditional on a hazard that was never fitted against it. Nine points do
not locate an optimum finer than their spacing (0.6 and 0.75 are 0.562
vs 0.573). And a hand-set scalar shared by two species is itself the
kind of per-clade constant the charter wants removed — this buys
accuracy now and hands the real problem, calibrating the term without a
knob, to the fitting.
