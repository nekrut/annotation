## Publication addition and benchmark scope

Brůna et al. (2023), [Galba: genome annotation with miniprot and AUGUSTUS](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-023-05449-z), describes target-genome training of AUGUSTUS from miniprot/miniprothint protein alignments, followed by evidence-assisted prediction. Its multi-species application therefore includes target-specific adaptation. The benchmark uses softmasked genomes and selected related proteomes; identical protein inputs are supplied to GALBA and BRAKER2.

Selected **Table 1 gene F1 percentages against the full references**:

| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Arabidopsis thaliana | 75.32 | 78.20 |
| Caenorhabditis elegans | 53.51 | 70.71 |

| Gallus gallus | 43.03 | 23.92 |
| Mus musculus | 37.62 | 27.80 |
| Tetraodon nigroviridis | 9.52 | 9.80 |

The paper reports Drosophila wall times of 3 h 24 min for GALBA and 3 h 3 min for BRAKER2 on 48 Xeon E5-2650 v4 threads. GALBA without metaparameter optimization took 44 min. These are reported pipeline times on prepared inputs, not local measurements or a repeat-library construction budget. [Final article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12859-023-05449-z).

## Reliable-subset sensitivity is not F1

[Supplementary Tables S3/S4](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1186%2Fs12859-023-05449-z/MediaObjects/12859_2023_5449_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) explicitly report **sensitivity**. S3 selects multi-exon genes with RNA-seq support for all introns; S4 selects gene structures agreed upon by multiple annotations. S4 contains:

| Target | GALBA gene sensitivity | GALBA exon sensitivity | BRAKER2 gene sensitivity |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| D. rerio | 70.16 | 93.49 | 58.78 |
| G. gallus | 72.00 | 94.08 | 30.16 |
| M. musculus | 77.85 | 95.18 | 40.31 |

The main Results prose calls the high scores in these subsets exon/gene F1. The cited supplementary tables do not supply corresponding precision or F1. Preserve their sensitivity labels; do not replace the full-reference F1 results with them. Selecting supported reference genes also changes the evaluated population.

S5 lists GALBA 1.0.6, AUGUSTUS 3.5.0, BRAKER 3.0.0, miniprot `0.9-r224-dirty`, miniprothint `a38f300`, and boundary scorer `37493bc`. The dirty miniprot identifier leaves a historical reconstruction gap. S3.6 combines outputs with TSEBRA's `--keep_gtf galba.gtf`, which makes this particular ensemble asymmetric. [Supplement, S5 and S3.6](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1186%2Fs12859-023-05449-z/MediaObjects/12859_2023_5449_MOESM1_ESM.pdf).

## Table arithmetic and scorer provenance

I parsed all Table 1 cells from the [final article XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10472564/fullTextXML) and independently extracted the [final PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s12859-023-05449-z.pdf). All 180 numerical cells agree between representations: 168 species scores plus 12 displayed averages. Seven averages match arithmetic means of the fourteen displayed species values, rounded with decimal arithmetic. Five do not:

| Method / endpoint | Displayed average | Computed mean of displayed species scores |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| GALBA / transcript | 35.23 | 35.30 |
| BRAKER2 / gene | 42.05 | 41.67 |
| BRAKER2 / transcript | 35.42 | 34.88 |
| BRAKER2 / exon | 70.41 | 70.95 |
| TSEBRA G+B / exon | 73.35 | 74.78 |

These are arithmetic checks of published values, not rescored predictions. The discrepancies exceed rounding of the individual values; an alternative aggregation or underlying revision was not established. Use species rows when synthesizing this comparison.

The paper names BRAKER's `compute_accuracies.sh`. In the [v3.0.0 script](https://github.com/Gaius-Augustus/BRAKER/blob/ef291073ca81fc873a2079cbe55402c885443bf4/scripts/compute_accuracies.sh), it calls `compare_intervals_exact.pl`, not GffCompare. The [comparator](https://github.com/Gaius-Augustus/BRAKER/blob/ef291073ca81fc873a2079cbe55402c885443bf4/scripts/compare_intervals_exact.pl#L790) builds CDS keys from sequence, both boundaries, strand and, by default, phase; transcript matching uses the complete CDS-key sequence. Gene matching credits a matching transcript structure. The wrapper passes pseudogene coordinates, and the comparator excludes predictions overlapping eligible pseudogene regions after protecting reference-overlapping regions. These are static observations at the declared BRAKER release; the precise historical scorer checkout and input normalization were not reconstructed.

## Current default filtering changes the comparison

At current GALBA head [`5729728de1c28c0386c866a283ef0c33f10e46fc`](https://github.com/Gaius-Augustus/GALBA/tree/5729728de1c28c0386c866a283ef0c33f10e46fc), `disable_diamond_filter` defaults to zero. The [postprocessing call](https://github.com/Gaius-Augustus/GALBA/blob/5729728de1c28c0386c866a283ef0c33f10e46fc/scripts/galba.pl#L5331) therefore filters predictions against the input proteins. That flag/helper is absent from the [paper's v1.0.6 pipeline](https://github.com/Gaius-Augustus/GALBA/blob/f96a026c03fa08cfe728c1ef230413b651e28cb0/scripts/galba.pl).

The [filter implementation](https://github.com/Gaius-Augustus/GALBA/blob/5729728de1c28c0386c866a283ef0c33f10e46fc/scripts/filter_gtf_by_diamond_against_ref.py#L55) retains transcripts with a DIAMOND row and their parent genes; an unsupported sibling isoform is removed. It does not apply another identity/coverage threshold after DIAMOND reports a hit. DIAMOND's own search settings still determine reported hits.

Three isolated checks passed using only the extracted `filter_gtf` function and in-memory streams: one supported isoform retains its parent but removes its sibling; no hits produces no retained models; hits in separate genes retain the corresponding transcripts. The synthetic low-quality row tests the filter's membership logic, not whether DIAMOND would emit that row. No aligner, pipeline, installation or genome inference ran.

**Benchmark/design implication:** pin the final filtering policy as well as the predictor and donor proteomes. Evaluate sensitivity for genes without supplied homologs separately. A hard homology filter can remove the very predictions intended to extend beyond protein evidence. Compare current filtered and unfiltered outputs with the same scorer and report target-adaptation costs.
