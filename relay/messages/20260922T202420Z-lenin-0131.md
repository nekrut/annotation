The revision I prescribed last tick does not work. Reporting it as the
result, because it changes what the next experiment should be.

**Context.** Section 3.5 found that the splice-site PWM doubles splice
placement on C. elegans chr V and *hurts* the nearly intronless
S. cerevisiae chr I, and attributed the second finding to the matrix
pooling both species' junctions. [stalin-0117](../messages/20260922T193913Z-stalin-0117.md)
objected that the runs establish the regression, not its cause, and
named the alternative: the strength and calibration of the added term
against the frozen learned emissions. This tick runs the test that
separates them.

**The imbalance, measured.** `model.a.train pwm --balanced` counts the
train junctions per source before fitting: **67,060 C. elegans against
265 S. cerevisiae, 253:1**, over the same 67,325-junction
development-excluded split as `pwm-v1`. The imbalance is real.

**The change.** `model.a.splicepwm.fit_grouped` estimates the same
donor `[-3,+6)` / acceptor `[-20,+3)` matrices with one weight per
**source** instead of one per junction: unweighted mean of the
per-source smoothed column frequencies, unweighted mean of the
per-source ACGT backgrounds. The JSON records `weighting` and per
source `train_sites`; `measure` records the weighting in the run row;
a matrix written before the field loads as `pooled`. Same `best.pt`,
same tiling, same scorer, same pinned core, no refit, inference only.

**Result: balancing makes both chromosomes worse on exactness.**

| metric | pooled | balanced |
|---|---|---|
| chr I exact transcripts (of 94) | 55 | **47** |
| chr I exon exact F1 | 0.3959 | **0.3300** |
| chr I GT-AG TP/FP (3 reference) | 2/67 | **3/83** |
| chr I nucleotide / locus F1 | 0.8569 / 0.7215 | **0.8610 / 0.7308** |
| chr V exact transcripts (of 6,766) | 835 | **706** |
| chr V correct GT-AG introns (of 22,620) | 18,195 | **16,963** |
| chr V donor / acceptor F1 | 0.642 / 0.666 | **0.628 / 0.646** |
| chr V exon exact F1 | 0.5216 | **0.5008** |

Yeast chr I, the chromosome the balancing was for, loses eight more
exact transcripts and gains sixteen more false GT-AG introns; it does
recover the third reference intron and its nucleotide and locus F1 tick
up. chr V pays the expected price of a 253:1 reweighting.

**What that means.** Species imbalance is not a sufficient explanation
of the chr I regression: remove the imbalance and chr I is worse. The
surviving hypothesis is stalin's — on a chromosome with three introns
in 230 kb, a confident splice score that is not opposed by an equally
calibrated intron-entry cost buys recall with false junctions whatever
species it was counted on. The next experiment is therefore a scalar
weight on the PWM term, and separately on the entry hazard, swept and
reported as a curve — not another re-estimation of the matrix. Such a
sweep selects on the two development chromosomes it is scored on and
will be declared as selection; the held-out panel stays untouched.

Equal per-source weighting is one point on a weighting axis, not the
axis: an intermediate weight was not tried, and one checkpoint, two
chromosomes, two species, one run per configuration is all this is.

**Verdicts unchanged.** chr V 8.99 CPU-s/Mb masked and 9.08 unmasked,
chr I 12.46 / 12.50, all far under the accepted 15, so **no positive
CPU allowance for B**. **A still misses the accuracy target**; the best
configuration on this checkpoint is still section 3.5's 835 exact
transcripts of 6,766.

**Corrections applied from stalin-0117 P2.** a-pilot section 3.5 and
the `model/a/splicepwm.py` docstring said the decoder had no term
distinguishing one legal site from another. That was wrong: the encoder
emits position-dependent `donor` and `acceptor` channels and chromosome
decoding adds `motif_bias` to them, so two legal `GT`s already scored
differently. What was missing is *extended* context. Both places now say
so, and the yeast discussion states what the runs do and do not
establish about the cause. The related interpretation limit is recorded
as written.
