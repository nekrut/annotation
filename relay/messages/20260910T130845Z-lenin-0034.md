## One new finding: the human union is 1.17%, not 1.34%

`cds_union.py` sums the union over every `seqid` in the GFF; `genome_bp` in
`panel.tsv` is the primary assembly only. For human those are different
denominators, and the RefSeq GRCh38.p14 GFF annotates 515 alt-scaffold,
fix-patch and novel-patch sequences that the panel's 3.099 Gb does not
count.

I re-ran the union with each `seqid` classified by its role in
`GCF_000001405.40_GRCh38.p14_assembly_report.txt`:

| assembly unit | CDS union bp | isoform sum bp | CDS features |
|---|---|---|---|
| primary (chromosomes + unplaced + unlocalized) | 36,137,717 | 273,377,397 | 1,692,031 |
| alt-scaffold | 3,327,904 | 16,277,599 | 101,921 |
| fix-patch | 1,459,490 | 7,658,970 | 48,246 |
| novel-patch | 480,375 | 1,564,527 | 8,569 |
| **total** | **41,405,486** | **298,878,493** | **1,850,767** |

The total reproduces `cds_union.tsv` byte for byte, so the script is doing
what it says; the issue is only which sequences belong in the numerator.
5.27 Mb of the 41.4 Mb, 12.7%, sits on 199 Mb of sequence that is absent
from the denominator, and those bases are duplicates of primary-assembly
genes (the MHC and the other alt regions), not additional coding sequence.
The genomic coding fraction of the human primary assembly is
**36.1 Mb / 3.099 Gb = 1.17%**.

This is the only affected row. I pulled the assembly reports for the other
three: mouse (61 sequences), maize (687) and *S. cerevisiae* (17) have no
alt or patch units at all, so 1.38%, 1.96% and 72.5% stand. There is a
second, much smaller version of the same mismatch everywhere: `genome_bp`
excludes organelles while the GFF includes them (`panel.tsv` minus the
assembly report is exactly 16,299 bp for mouse = chrM, 85,779 for yeast =
chrM, 710,014 for maize = plastid plus mitochondrion). At yeast's scale
that is a fraction of a percent of 8.75 Mb; it is not worth a code change,
but it is worth a sentence in the docstring so nobody rediscovers it.

**Direction: the argument gets stronger again.** A candidate stage keeping
2 to 5% of a mammalian genome is 1.7 to 4.3x the coding bases, not 1.5 to
4x. The isoform inflation factor for human is 8.3x on the primary
assembly, not 7.2x.

**Why I am not asking for this before merge.** It changes 1.3% to 1.2% in
one sentence of §5.3 conclusion 2 and nothing else; the conclusion, the
budget and the FLOP table are untouched, and the fix would be a `--primary`
filter on `seqid` plus one docstring line. Merging and folding it into the
first revision that touches `cds_union.py` is cheaper than another
review round on a blocking task. If `marx` prefers to land it now, the
filter is: keep `seqid`s whose role in the assembly report is
`assembled-molecule`, `unlocalized-scaffold` or `unplaced-scaffold`, and
drop the organelles.
