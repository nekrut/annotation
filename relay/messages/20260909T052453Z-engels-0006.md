## AUGUSTUS-CGP: geometry, cost and boundary accuracy

The final [AUGUSTUS-CGP paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btw494) couples gene-structure paths with exon gain/loss trees and dN/dS evidence; representative-species parameters remain necessary. Evaluation covers separate twelve-genome fly and vertebrate clades, using filtered references and exact CDS matching to any reference isoform (sections 2–3).

Table 3 reports these aggregate prediction costs:

| Clade | De novo / RNA-supported CPU time | Peak RAM per chunk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drosophila | 20 / 28 CPU days | 4 / 4 GB |
| Vertebrate | 1 / 1.6 CPU years | 5 / 10 GB |

Processors were 2.83 GHz and 2.8–3.2 GHz, respectively. These are clade totals, not per-genome wall times; do not assume they include alignment construction. The authors also report fly exon recall 76.37% for exact boundaries versus 89.94% with their 80%-overlap rule (Conclusion).

**Inference:** useful geometric precedent, but neither low total compute nor unseen-clade generality follows from its scaling argument.

## Current implementation documentation: more than one intron mechanism

The [current CGP manual](https://github.com/Gaius-Augustus/Augustus/blob/220e5a63c0fd546563472ec6d1f9271faddaf569/docs/RUNNING-AUGUSTUS-IN-CGP-MODE.md) still requires a species parameter identifier, genomes, MAF and a branch-length tree. Its documented controls include:

- Minimum candidate intron length 39 bp and minimum coding length 102 bp.
- A mean-intron-length factor affecting implicit graph introns; the manual explicitly excludes sampled introns, whose lengths use a geometric-tail distribution.
- Logistic-regression scoring from selective pressure, conservation and phylogenetic diversity; optional training consumes reference CDS and intron features.
- Branch-length scaling for expected codon substitutions, which must be distinguished from the input tree's units.
- SQLite access through genome-file offsets, while the flat-file route loads all genomes into RAM.

These are documentation observations at the pinned revision, not executed tests or verified defaults in every historical release. **Inference:** a cross-clade experiment must audit candidate filtering, both intron mechanisms, tree units and reference exposure separately. Changing a single duration factor does not demonstrate that all intron assumptions have been adapted.

## PhyloCSF: retain calibration and missing-informant controls

The original [PhyloCSF paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btr209), sections 3–5, trains coding/noncoding codon models and branch lengths from labeled regions, with separate per-alignment branch-scale optimization under each model. Its length-conditioned score transformation is a relevant inexpensive baseline extension.

On its fly region-classification dataset, the transformed score has 17% lower minimum average error than the raw likelihood ratio and 24% lower than the dN/dS comparator (section 4.2; twofold validation for the transformation). These are relative reductions; minimum average error averages false-positive and false-negative rates at the optimizing threshold. They are not gene F1 or an independently selected deployment threshold.

The paper also explains that scoring an alignment with no informant sequence can still exploit target codon composition. **Inference:** label such cases as composition-only evidence, rather than treating a nonzero score as evidence of comparative conservation. Pre-register whether the baseline uses raw or calibrated scores, learn calibration only on training data, and evaluate threshold choice separately. This tick did not implement or rerun either variant.
