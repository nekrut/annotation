## geneML: fungal holdout, evidence configuration and scoring

The [May 21, 2026 preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.18.725946) describes a SpliceAI-derived CNN, per-base classes and constrained recursive gene construction. Its benchmark excludes all nine evaluation genera from geneML training and the OrthoDB protein evidence supplied to the BRAKER executable. This supports genus holdout within fungi, not unseen-clade or pan-eukaryotic transfer. Its internal 90/10 partition is used for stopping/model selection; it should be called validation separately from the external benchmark.

I parsed [Supplementary Table S1's workbook](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2026/05/21/2026.05.18.725946/DC2/embed/media-2.xlsx?download=true) directly with standard-library ZIP/XML readers: 765 assembly rows, four general synonym exclusions, 761 retained genomes, then nine benchmark-genus exclusions, leaving 752. All nine relevant genus rows carry the benchmark-exclusion note. An initial filter recognized semicolon-separated exclusions but missed a comma-separated one; the corrected count handles both. This checks the manifest, not the actual training stream or checkpoint identity.

[Supplementary Table S5](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2026/05/21/2026.05.18.725946/DC1/embed/media-1.pdf?download=true) specifies GffCompare 0.12.10 with `--no-exon-merge --strict-match -e 0`. The zero terminal tolerance matters: the command addresses the terminal-coordinate issue in earlier Helixer/ANNEVO addenda. GffCompare's [documentation](https://ccb.jhu.edu/software/stringtie/gffcompare.shtml) still defines loci through exon-overlap clustering, rather than annotation gene IDs. The exact executable and phase/stop normalization were not tested here.

The supplement's hardware description is dual Xeon Silver 4410Y, 125 GB installed RAM, Ubuntu 22.04, sequential isolated containers restricted to eight CPUs and one NUMA node. Installed RAM is not peak usage. S5 lists BRAKER v3.0.8 with protein input and no RNA-seq input; this is not the RNA-plus-protein configuration of the BRAKER3 paper. Record the configuration with the result.

I parsed all 135 S6 accuracy rows and recomputed the 45 main-table means: every difference was below 0.05 percentage points. Averaging all 45 S8 rounded runtime rows reproduces the five main-table means within 0.031 minutes. This verifies published-table arithmetic, not prediction accuracy.

Unresolved preprint inconsistencies: the abstract gives geneML/AUGUSTUS Iso-Seq recall/precision as 41.1/71.1 and 33.8/48.9; Table 4 and Results give 41.2/71.1 and 33.8/48.0. Methods names the YL1 genome, whereas Table 4's caption names PH-1. Preserve the discrepancies until the actual Iso-Seq inputs are identified. Comparator training overlap is acknowledged in the preprint; this was not an equal-training-data experiment.

## geneML source: an explicit intron ceiling and bounded search

Source is pinned to the existing inventory revision [0627add6269cd9b9e4c0028748e6802513c6e2d6](https://github.com/hexagonbio/geneML/tree/0627add6269cd9b9e4c0028748e6802513c6e2d6).

- [args.py](https://github.com/hexagonbio/geneML/blob/0627add6269cd9b9e4c0028748e6802513c6e2d6/src/geneml/args.py) defaults to introns of 10–400 bases, at most five transcripts and 5,000 gene candidates.
- [gene_caller.py](https://github.com/hexagonbio/geneML/blob/0627add6269cd9b9e4c0028748e6802513c6e2d6/src/geneml/gene_caller.py), lines 327–335, rejects an exon connection above the configured intron maximum. This is a hard decoder constraint, not merely a learned preference. Other pruning and operation limits also restrict candidates. Raising the intron flag does not establish long-intron accuracy or unchanged runtime.
- [trainer/train_model.py](https://github.com/hexagonbio/geneML/blob/0627add6269cd9b9e4c0028748e6802513c6e2d6/trainer/train_model.py), lines 362–368, randomly partitions dataset indices and evaluates a fixed validation subset. That code alone does not establish locus/family separation; the external genus exclusion is a separate protection.
- [outputs.py](https://github.com/hexagonbio/geneML/blob/0627add6269cd9b9e4c0028748e6802513c6e2d6/src/geneml/outputs.py) emits exon and CDS rows at identical coordinates, with phase on CDS. UTR prediction is absent.

Design inference: geneML is relevant prior work for a compact local encoder plus explicit graph/path construction. Its fast fungal result does not remove the need to measure candidate recall across intron regimes.

## Original Helixer: the metric and development-set exposure

The [original Helixer paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa1044), sections 2.1–2.4 and Table 3, fills the draft's prototype-result gap. Its final ensemble has median Subgenic F1 0.868 for animals and 0.863 for plants, versus 0.632/0.757 for its BUSCO-trained AUGUSTUS comparator. Subgenic F1 pools base-level counts for CDS/intron classes; it is not gene or transcript F1. The prototype outputs per-base probabilities, without coherent GFF gene structures.

The paper initially reserves 19 animal and six plant test genomes, but reports combined evaluation-plus-test performance. Evaluation genomes repeatedly influenced training-species selection and architecture decisions. **Inference:** these aggregate scores are not a wholly untouched species-test estimate. Preserve a separate final-test-only endpoint when synthesizing this study. The vertebrate and plant models were trained separately; algae and non-avian reptiles are explicitly identified as weak-transfer cases.
