## SNAP: quantify transfer with its evaluation filters

[SNAP (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-5-59), Table 3, reports the following exact-gene recall/precision percentages; the paper calls precision specificity.

| Parameter species | Target species | Recall | Precision |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Arabidopsis | Arabidopsis | 54.3 | 46.8 |
| Rice | Arabidopsis | 5.1 | 6.1 |
| Rice | Drosophila | 27.2 | 17.2 |
| Arabidopsis | C. elegans | 20.9 | 11.3 |
| Drosophila | C. elegans | 7.8 | 6.9 |

Native rows use fivefold cross-validation. Methods remove alternative isoforms, overlapping genes, noncanonical splice sites, split start/stop codons, and introns shorter than 30 bp. Full-length cDNA supports retained genes; proteins exceeding 90% identity are deduplicated. Flanks extend at most 1 kb.

Table 4 uses default architectures and nucleotide metrics, whereas Table 3 uses tuned architectures. The often-cited 21.7 value is rice-to-C. elegans **nucleotide sensitivity** in Table 4, not gene sensitivity. C. elegans-to-Arabidopsis nucleotide recall/precision changes from 86.3/91.0 to 96.6/93.2 after bootstrapping (Table 4).

**Inference:** transfer is composition-sensitive, but these filtered loci leave difficult transcript structures and long intergenic background largely untested.

## AUGUSTUS: retain the manuscript version and reference denominators

The [author-hosted manuscript](https://gobics.de/mario/papers/GenePred2003.pdf), dated July 17, 2003 ([journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btg1080)), supplies these percentages in Tables 2–4:

| Dataset | Exon recall/precision | Gene recall/precision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| h178 | 80/80 | 46/45 |
| sag178 | 78/71 | 40/35 |
| adh222 | 86/66 | 71/39 |

sag178 embeds h178 genes in synthetic intergenic sequence. adh222 recall uses the smaller std1 reference; precision uses std3. **Inference:** do not compute a conventional matched-reference F1 from that pair.

Table 4's caption says 75/178 genes correct, inconsistent with its 40% sensitivity (75/178 is approximately 42.1%). Preserve the discrepancy pending journal reconciliation.

Table 5 gives intron-model gains of 0.3 human and 3.4 fly points on a weighted arithmetic mean of exon/gene recall/precision differences, not F1. The model uses explicit short-intron lengths and a geometric tail; author-reported runtime is approximately six minutes for 1.6 Mb on a 2.4 GHz PC. These historical manuscript results were not rerun.

## GeneMark-ES 2005: unlabeled adaptation and internal-exon endpoints

[The original study](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gki937), Table 2, reports internal-exon recall/precision of 91.4/95.4% for C. reinhardtii and 80.2/83.1% for T. gondii. Their test sets contain 43 and 65 genes, respectively. The adjacent 93.4 and 81.7 table entries are arithmetic means, not F1.

Unsupervised training iteratively parses the target genome and refits parameters. Its stopping rule requires agreement above 97% between successive parses, not 97% agreement with true annotations. Supervised training/test separation is documented separately; it does not establish exclusion of test DNA from unsupervised input.

Methods select canonical, transcript-supported genes without annotated alternative isoforms or overlapping genes. The minimum-size experiment changes the accepted training-CDS threshold below 10 Mb, from 800 to 300 nt. Thus its training-size curve changes both data quantity and filtering.

**Inference:** classify this as adaptation to unlabeled target sequence, distinct from a frozen model evaluated on unseen species. Audit adaptation inputs and annotation exposure separately.

## GeneMark-ES 2008: an intron motif mechanism beyond duration alone

[The fungal extension](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.081612.108) ([institutional full text](https://repository.gatech.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/36302305-aba3-41f1-8afa-a60297c3078d/content)) combines intron paths with and without a branch-point model and estimates their use through self-training.

Table 2 compares original/enhanced intron models. M. grisea internal-exon recall/precision rises from 70.8/84.0% to 89.2/91.7%; C. cinereus rises from 81.5/87.9% to 85.0/89.7%. These are conditional internal-exon results on type-I complete-gene tests, not whole-genome gene F1.

Methods explicitly warn that translation start/stop reference positions were determined computationally. Other species use incomplete-gene tests. Results describe weak branch-point signals and pronounced polypyrimidine tails in R. oryzae, despite its short introns.

**Inference:** stratify intron modeling by splice-signal regime as well as length. Reference support for an intron chain should not automatically validate exact CDS termini. Frozen cross-clade transfer remains unestablished by this per-genome self-training study.
