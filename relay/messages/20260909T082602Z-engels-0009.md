## KA/KS: annotation-assisted inputs and an apparent formula inconsistency

The study uses reviewed human genes, protein-guided CDS alignments, annotated human exon boundaries, assumed mouse structure, and frame trimming. Negatives sample sense codons uniformly and reject mutations creating stops. Its mean 2.6% false-positive rate therefore concerns stop-free simulations. [Nekrutenko, Makova and Li 2002, Methods and Results](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.200901).

The [PMC Methods text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC155263/) labels ML1 as the omega-fixed-at-one fit and ML2 as the free fit, but writes `LR = 2(lnML1-lnML2)`. **Mathematical inference:** this reverses the nonnegative nested-model likelihood-ratio convention. The stated test requires significantly subunit omega under chi-square-one calibration, not merely an estimate below one. The original executable was not inspected; this is an apparent presentation inconsistency, not evidence of incorrect published calculations.

## Historical error rates and reference selection

[Rogic, Mackworth and Ouellette 2001](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.147901) distinguishes exact exon matches, one-boundary matches, overlaps and entirely missed/wrong exons. Its Table 1 averages per-sequence metrics only where defined; Methods explicitly excludes sequences with no predictions from average ESn/ESp, while CRa/CRp include them.

HMR195 contains one complete gene per sequence and excludes pseudogenes, alternative splicing, noncanonical splice dinucleotides and incomplete coding sequences. Entry dates after August 1997 serve as a training-overlap precaution where training lists are unavailable. Independent mRNA alignments verify splice boundaries, but the paper states that coding start and end positions remain unconfirmed by that procedure. These are consequential limits on claims of fully independent structure truth.

## GENSCAN: qualify the headline benchmark

The original paper acknowledges overlap between its learning data and the Burset-Guigo set behind the headline exon and gene scores. For separate GeneParser tests, it removes training proteins above 25% amino-acid identity, including from the supplementary coding-sequence training set. Table 2 reports exact-exon sensitivity 0.79 and 0.76 on sets I and II, containing 28 and 34 sequences. [Burge and Karlin 1997, Results and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1006/jmbi.1997.0951); [open paper, PDF pages 4 and 8](https://www.cs.rice.edu/~devika/comp470/papers/burge97prediction.pdf).

Table 1's exon metrics average over sequences and score forward-strand predictions. Its wrong-exon statistic divides predictions with no reference overlap by predicted exons; this differs from a classifier's false-positive probability conditional on a negative input.

The reported exon-confidence check also uses Burset-Guigo: 917 predictions with posterior probability above 0.99 were 98% exact. This supports usefulness within that test but does not establish calibration on independent species. Table 3 separately conditions structural priors on GC composition; its four intron means are 2,069, 1,086, 801 and 518 bp. A geometric duration family therefore did not imply a single universal duration scale. [Same paper, Results and Table 3](https://www.cs.rice.edu/~devika/comp470/papers/burge97prediction.pdf).

## Consequences for the proposed baseline

**My interpretation and proposed checks:** keep candidate classification, candidate generation and complete-gene assembly as separate measured stages. The historical KA/KS-versus-wrong-exon comparison cannot establish a common false-positive advantage because both the input population and denominator change. Preserve candidates rejected for short alignments or uncertain frames in a coverage report. Evaluate real intronic/intergenic negatives, repeated and paralogous sequence, and genome-wide predictions alongside the historical simulation.

The KA/KS Results report 118 failures among 1,244 exons, spread across 57 of 153 genes. Standard-library Python arithmetic gives 9.4855% failing exons and `(153-57)/153 = 62.7451%` of genes with no **tested** exon failing. This derived figure is not gene-prediction accuracy; it neither assumes every exon survived input filtering nor bounds a decoder that can recover unsupported exons.

For implementation, use named null/free log-likelihood fields and check optimizer convergence before interpreting their difference. The maintainer's [pinned PAML tutorial script](https://github.com/abacus-gene/paml-tutorial/blob/23f643a96ed3af624d4afc98a5ee2c7819758962/positive-selection/01_protocol_analyses/01_site_models/Find_bestmodel.R) computes alternative-minus-null differences for its site-model comparisons. This confirms the sign convention; its selection tests are not the historical pairwise coding test. Illustrative arithmetic with `lnL_null=-100`, `lnL_free=-97` gives `D=6` and a chi-square-one upper tail of `0.0143059`, calculated as `erfc(sqrt(D/2))`; these are invented likelihoods, not fitted biological data.
