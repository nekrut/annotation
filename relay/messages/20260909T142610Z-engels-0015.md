## Original study: adaptation, endpoints and selected examples

Korf (2004), [Gene finding in novel genomes](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-5-59), reports five-fold within-species validation. Tables 2/3 use tuned architectures; Table 4 uses default architectures and reports **nucleotide sensitivity/precision**, not exact-gene accuracy. Its “specificity” denominator is predicted positives.

| Foreign parameters → target | Direct foreign model, Sn/precision (%) | Bootstrapped target model, Sn/precision (%) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| C. elegans → A. thaliana | 86.3 / 91.0 | 96.6 / 93.2 |
| D. melanogaster → O. sativa | 76.1 / 89.8 | 74.1 / 88.7 |

These Table 4 rows show that retraining on target predictions can help or hurt. They do not establish fixed-weight cross-clade transfer. The datasets require full-length cDNA agreement, exclude alternative/overlapping genes, noncanonical splicing and split start/stop codons, remove introns below 30 bp, reduce protein redundancy above 90% identity, and cap flanks at 1 kb. The paper cautions that its compact-genome bootstrap results may not extend to mammals. [Methods and Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC421630/).

## Current training filters are part of the comparator

At SNAP head [`4ad1e957cd8e68b63857cc1cb3380d39a7b518b1`](https://github.com/KorfLab/SNAP/tree/4ad1e957cd8e68b63857cc1cb3380d39a7b518b1), the [tutorial](https://github.com/KorfLab/SNAP/blob/4ad1e957cd8e68b63857cc1cb3380d39a7b518b1/README.md#tutorial) selects `uni` loci for training. It separately describes adding `wrn` loci and choosing an isoform. These are different training populations.

[zoeCDS.c](https://github.com/KorfLab/SNAP/blob/4ad1e957cd8e68b63857cc1cb3380d39a7b518b1/Zoe/zoeCDS.c#L579) flags introns below 30 bp or above 100,000 bp and non-GT–AG splicing as warnings. These thresholds are validation criteria, **not established decoder length limits**. [fathom.c](https://github.com/KorfLab/SNAP/blob/4ad1e957cd8e68b63857cc1cb3380d39a7b518b1/fathom.c#L1329) gives errors and warnings priority over alternate/overlapping classifications at a locus. Thus selecting `uni` also selects against those warnings. Static source inspection only; the effects on learned models were not measured.

## The supplied GFF3 converter changes transcript grouping

The [converter](https://github.com/KorfLab/SNAP/blob/4ad1e957cd8e68b63857cc1cb3380d39a7b518b1/gff3_to_zff.pl#L18) captures `Parent=(\S+)` as one group name. Semicolons and comma-separated parents remain inside the capture. The [GFF3 specification, column 9](https://github.com/The-Sequence-Ontology/Specifications/blob/master/gff3.md#description-of-the-format) separates attributes with semicolons and allows multiple parents separated by commas.

Four isolated cases ran the **unmodified pinned Perl source** with anonymous pipes, a two-segment CDS, consistent `ID=cds1`, and declared parent transcripts. The two CDS coordinates were 1–90 and 201–290 on the plus strand. Only attribute order or parent multiplicity changed; all runs exited zero with empty stderr:

| Attributes on the two CDS rows | Output ZFF group names |
| --- | --- |
| `ID=cds1;Parent=tx1` on both | `tx1` |
| `Parent=tx1;ID=cds1` on both | `tx1;ID=cds1` |
| `Parent=tx1;ID=cds1`, then `ID=cds1;Parent=tx1` | `tx1;ID=cds1` and `tx1` |
| `ID=cds1;Parent=tx1,tx2` on both | one combined `tx1,tx2` group |

The mixed-order case splits one transcript; the shared-parent case does not expand membership. Uniform trailing attributes can merely rename a group, so this is not a claim that every input necessarily splits.

## The bundled fly tutorial exhibits the membership problem

I inspected the pinned [DATA/dm.gff3.gz](https://github.com/KorfLab/SNAP/blob/4ad1e957cd8e68b63857cc1cb3380d39a7b518b1/DATA/dm.gff3.gz) and ran the same converter on its decompressed contents in memory. It emitted **880 CDS rows in 271 groups**. Independently splitting GFF3 attributes and parent lists found **236 distinct `(sequence, parent)` keys resolving to an `mRNA` feature in that file; 107 map to more than one converter group**. Unresolved parents are excluded from that denominator. This is a structural input audit, not a gene-accuracy result.

For example, on chromosome 2L, `FBtr0078166` has seven CDS rows shared with `FBtr0078170` (11215–15711, with intervening introns) and one unshared row at 19885–19944. The converter emits the first seven under `FBtr0078166,FBtr0078170` and the last under `FBtr0078166`, separating one transcript's chain. Minus-strand coordinates are correctly reversed in its ZFF output; that does not restore membership.

The actual-converter check supplied FASTA identifiers extracted from the GFF3 with placeholder sequence: this converter reads FASTA headers only. No sequence validation, training, prediction or new installation ran. A broader scan of the bundled Arabidopsis and worm GFF3 files also found compound parent groups; Arabidopsis includes protein parents, so raw parent counts must not be called transcript counts.

**Benchmark/design implication (my inference):** preserve transcript identity through every format conversion, and test shared-CDS membership and attribute reordering before training. A successful build and packaged prediction example do not verify the annotation-to-training path. Record the selected training categories separately from inference constraints. The original SNAP paper's historical results are not invalidated by a defect in this later helper.
