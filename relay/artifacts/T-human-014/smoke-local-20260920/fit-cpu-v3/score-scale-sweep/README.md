# score-scale-sweep/: how loud should the splice PWM be? 2026-09-22

Section 3.7 of `docs/design/a-pilot.md`. The experiment sections 3.5 and
3.6 pointed at: the same `best.pt`, the same 19-segment tiling, the same
scorer, the same pinned pooled matrix (`../pwm-v1/splicepwm.json`,
sha256 `a964e3e6…`), the accepted finite-score support (no hard mask) —
and one scalar multiplying every log-odds entry of that matrix, so the
matrix fixes the *shape* of the junction preference and the scalar sets
how loud it is against the frozen learned emissions and the intron-entry
hazard it competes with. Inference only, no refit, weights unchanged.

Nine points. `scale-000/` … `scale-200/` were run here by
`run_scale.sh`; scale 1.0 is `../score-pwm-only/`, already committed, and
is listed in the curve under its own directory.

The selection criterion was fixed before the points above 1.0 were run:
the **unweighted mean of exon-exact F1 over the two development
chromosomes**, unweighted so that the 20.9 Mb nematode cannot outvote
the 0.23 Mb yeast on a species-independence question. It picks
**0.75**. Selection is on development chromosomes only; no held-out
species was touched.

## What the curve says

1. **Scale 0 is the control and it is exact.** Both predictions at
   `scale-000/` are byte-identical to `../score-final/`'s
   (`predictions.sha256`; chr I `6cd5faab…`, chr V `5f649777…`), and the
   two score JSONs differ in exactly one top-level key, `declaration`,
   which states the scale. The scalar short-circuits to
   "no PWM" rather than approximating it, and widening the slice a tile
   scores changes nothing it reads.

2. **Scale 1.0 — every earlier PWM measurement — is past the peak on
   both chromosomes.** At 0.75 chr V gains 264 exact transcripts over
   scale 1.0 (830 → 1,094), exon-exact F1 0.51987 → 0.59758, nucleotide
   F1 0.76614 → 0.80815, locus F1 0.63826 → 0.71351, donor F1
   0.640 → 0.728, acceptor 0.664 → 0.763, fusions 740 → 576. It is
   bought entirely out of intron over-prediction: predicted introns
   38,908 → 25,637 against 22,620 reference GT-AG, false GT-AG
   20,082 → 8,863, nucleotide precision 0.643 → 0.723 against
   sensitivity 0.948 → 0.916.

3. **The species-independence failure of 3.5 was calibration, not
   imbalance.** The yeast collapse that section 3.5 reported and section
   3.6 failed to fix by reweighting is a property of the *strength* of
   the term: chr I holds 63–65 exact transcripts flat from scale 0 to
   0.75, then falls 57 → 55 → 12 → 0. False GT-AG introns on a
   chromosome with three real ones go 0, 12, 12, 16, 23, 39, 67, 551,
   1,133. Nothing about the matrix changed across these nine runs; only
   how loudly it was allowed to speak.

4. **Cost is flat.** 9.08–9.16 CPU-s/Mb on chr V and 12.39–12.82 on
   chr I across the whole sweep, monotone in scale only within run-to-run
   noise, and every point far under the accepted 15 CPU-s/Mb. **No
   positive CPU allowance for B follows from this section either.**

5. **A still misses the accuracy target.** The selected point is 1,094
   exact of 6,766 reference transcripts on chr V, transcript F1 0.193 as
   `benchmark/score.py` computes it (tp 1,094, fp 5,270, fn 3,901
   against matched loci), against 0.133 at scale 1.0 — the largest
   increment since 3.5, and still not acceptance.

## What it does not establish

One checkpoint, two development chromosomes, two species, one run per
point, no refit, no held-out species. The scalar is one parameter of a
calibration that has at least two — the term's strength and the
intron-entry hazard it competes with — and only the first was swept;
the peak's location is therefore conditional on a hazard that was never
fitted against it. A curve sampled at nine points does not locate an
optimum finer than its spacing, and 0.6 and 0.75 are within reach of
each other on the criterion (0.562 vs 0.573). The two chromosomes do not
agree on the argmax: 0.5 is the only point at which yeast is not worse
than no PWM at all (0.59193 vs 0.58182), and 0.75 costs yeast 0.033
exon-exact F1 to gain chr V 0.078. Reporting one operating point is a
choice under a declared criterion, not a free lunch.

## Layout and provenance

Each `scale-NNN/` holds `declaration.yaml` (the scale 1.0 declaration
with the scalar stated, so every scored point records which calibration
produced it), the two `measure_*.json` cost rows, the two `score_*.json`
accuracy rows, `/usr/bin/time -v` files for both measure and score
stages, the measure stdout, the two `seqids_*.txt`, and
`predictions.sha256`.

The chr V prediction is 2.6 MB per point and there are eight points
here. Every number in the curve comes from the committed JSONs; the
prediction is regenerated by `run_scale.sh <scale>`, which gates on the
fit's own recorded exit status and on the matrix digest before it runs.
So each point commits only the small chr I GFF3 and records the sha256
of both predictions in `predictions.sha256`. `scale-000/` commits
neither, because both of its predictions are the bytes already committed
under `../score-final/`.

Provenance: all sixteen measured rows record
`source_sha256 66e3e86a…` over the 20 files of `model/a` and
`model/grammar`, and `splice_pwm_sha256 a964e3e6…`. Scales 0, 0.25 and
0.5 were run before the scalar was committed and record
`commit 3fd9032` with `source_dirty: true`
(`chromosome.py`, `splicepwm.py`, `train.py`); scales 0.6 through 2.0
record `commit 2886763` with `source_dirty: false`. The source digest is
the same in both groups, so the first three points ran the bytes that
`2886763` later committed — that equality, not the commit field, is what
makes the curve one experiment. `summarize.py` regenerates the chain and
intron geometry quoted above for any point whose prediction has been
regenerated.
